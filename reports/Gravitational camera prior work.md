# Prior work surrounds a still-unclaimed gravitational camera

Within the limits of this research, nobody has proposed, analyzed or simulated a "gravitational camera" in the intended sense — a focal-plane-like array of idealized point gravitational-wave/tidal-strain detectors imaging everyday dynamic scenes, with a real-time engine showing the view — but every neighboring idea is occupied and the verdict is provisional. It is provisional because **no source was opened in full text**: arxiv.org and other hosts were blocked and a shared budget of 200 web searches ran out, so every citation below is confirmed only at abstract or search-snippet level, and "nothing found" means a general web search returned nothing, not that ADS, INSPIRE or Google Scholar were queried. The neighbors that must be cited are a US patent that already uses the words "gravity camera" for an array of static gravimeters, the Glasgow/NEWTON-g "multi-pixel gravity imager", R. M. L. Baker's high-frequency-gravitational-wave "imaging" proposals and the 2008 JASON study that dismissed them, D. Bar's all-gravitational-wave holography, and NASA Goddard's astrophysical "gravitational-wave imaging" framing. The physics to build on is also in print: LIGO's Newtonian-noise and Newtonian-calibrator literature shows that people, rotors and tumbleweeds register through their near-zone tidal field rather than through radiation; sixty years of calculations put the Sun's thermal graviton output near 10⁸ W while the textbook laboratory rotor radiates about 10⁻²⁹ W; Hawking's viscous damping rate, Peters' refractive index and the Smolin–Press–Garfinkle–Wald exchange describe how little matter does to a passing wave; and stochastic-background radiometry and pulsar-timing "imaging" supply spin-2 map-making mathematics, all of it far-field. What appears unclaimed is the camera and its engine plus a cluster of results the researchers derived but did not find in print: a near-zone resolution law Δx ≈ π × standoff / ln(SNR), a gravitational Kirchhoff law for ordinary condensed matter, a unified compactness bound on shadows, mirrors and lenses, and a tensor theory of image formation (a tensor coherence theorem, a point-pixel band limit at multipole l ≤ 4, an Ewald-sphere reading of the far-field formula, non-radiating stress distributions). Each absence claim rests on a handful of general web searches, often only one or two, several topics were never searched at all, and a handful of unread full texts (Smolin 1984/85, Press 1979, García-Cely & Ringwald, Nichols et al., Weinberg's textbook) could hold the "new" results as passing remarks, so the re-check tables that close this report are a precondition for any novelty claim in print.

## Nothing was read in full, so every claim carries a grade

The notes behind this report were compiled on 15 November 2026 by five research threads. Direct page fetches failed for every host tried (arxiv.org, ar5iv, ui.adsabs.harvard.edu, inspirehep.net, link.springer.com, patents.justia.com and rangevoting.org sit outside the session's network allowlist), and the shared search budget of 200 calls ran out mid-research: four threads logged 52, 41, 27 and 36 completed searches with at least 20 planned searches refused, and the fifth was cut off part-way. The report therefore uses three grades and keeps them apart.

A linked citation with no tag is **snippet-confirmed**: its title, URL and the stated content appeared in a search-result abstract or excerpt. The link points to the page the excerpt came from, not to a page anyone read, and where the excerpt came from a secondary source quoting the primary the text says so. The tag **[partial]** marks items whose URL and title were seen but whose bibliographic details were not. The tag **[derived]** marks the researchers' own arithmetic or short derivations, usually built on snippet-level formulas; these are not literature results and nobody has reviewed them independently. The tag **[recollection]** marks material recalled from background knowledge that nothing seen in the session supports; it appears only where explicitly flagged and in the closing re-check tables, and its journal references are themselves from memory. Phrases in quotation marks are as returned by search extracts and count as near-verbatim until checked against the source. A "negative result" always means that this web-search tool returned nothing relevant for the phrasing tried. Writing this synthesis added no new searches; the only computation run was the repository's own check scripts, which test arithmetic and algebra, not the literature.

## Four neighboring ideas exist, and none of them is the camera

The idea space around the project splits into four occupied corners: static Newtonian "gravity cameras", active imaging with laboratory-generated high-frequency gravitational waves (GWs), an all-GW holography formalism, and astrophysical "GW imaging". Fifty-two searches found nothing in the space between them: no physics treatment of image formation by sub-wavelength strain detectors viewing human-scale sources, no catalogue of what moving, heavy, hot or stressed objects look like in the strain channel, and no interactive engine that renders such images.

### "Gravity camera" and "multi-pixel gravity imager" already name static mass-mapping devices

The item a referee is most likely to hold up against the project's name is **US Patent 10,983,244, "Method for scanning an object using a gravimeter"**. Its text describes a scaled-up array of torsion-balance detectors as a "gravity camera" for imaging density profiles, claims that a sensitive version could in principle "see" a person walking behind hundreds of meters of concrete, and reports a simulation that locates a small sphere to 0.5 cm and its mass to 1% assuming 10 pGal precision ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10983244); [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10983244)) [partial: inventor, assignee and dates unconfirmed, and the attribution of the quoted passages to this patent number needs checking]. This is quasi-static Newtonian mass imaging and a patent claim of possibility, not hardware. The word "pixel" is taken as well: the Glasgow "Wee-g" MEMS gravimeter (Middlemiss et al., Nature 531, 2016) was motivated by "multi-pixel gravity imaging", and the NEWTON-g consortium aimed at "the first multi-pixel gravity imager" on Mount Etna ([TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2016-03-mems-technology-super-sensitive-gravity-detector.html); [EGU2020-1244](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-1244.html)). Demonstrated gravity imaging is coarse and static: the Birmingham cold-atom gradiometer found a 2 m × 2 m tunnel with signal-to-noise ratio 8, at 20 E statistical uncertainty and 0.5 m sampling along an 8.5 m line (Stray et al., Nature 602, 590, [Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/quantum-sensing-for-gravity-cartography)), and gravity gradiometry was proposed for imaging cargo containers in 2007 ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4215058/similar)). R. L. Forward's 1960s "gravitational mass sensors" at Hughes are the historical root of the line ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19660021148/downloads/19660021148.pdf)).

### Imaging objects with generated gravitational waves was proposed and officially dismissed

A 2004 US patent application titled "Gravitational wave imaging" describes a GW generator on one side of an object, a detector on the other, a display, and optional "gravitational wave lenses" ([Justia listing](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20040056545)) [partial: the inventor was not shown; the notes suspect R. M. L. Baker Jr., which is unverified]. Baker's 2007 conference report "Surveillance Applications of High-Frequency Gravitational Waves" proposed "a novel means of imaging" activity inside and below structures ([library record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_29615086)) [partial]. The October 2008 JASON study for the Director of National Intelligence concluded, as quoted by the Federation of American Scientists, that **"No foreign threat in HFGW is credible, including: communication by means of HFGW; object detection or imaging (by HFGW radar or tomography); vehicle propulsion by HFGW; or any other practical use of HFGW"** ([FAS](https://fas.org/blogs/secrecy/2008/12/jason_study)). A paper on a gravitational camera should cite that conclusion and say plainly that it presents an idealized thought experiment, not a feasibility claim. The one theoretical precedent that carries the word "holography" is D. Bar's "Gravitational wave holography", which shows that a subject wave can be reconstructed when subject, reference and reconstructing waves are all gravitational; it contains no detector array, no ordinary objects and no sensitivity analysis ([arXiv:gr-qc/0509052](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0509052)).

### In astronomy "imaging" means sky maps, and "pixel" means a patch of sky

NASA Goddard authors (Kelly, Thorpe, Baker and colleagues) told the 2022 APS April Meeting that nobody had yet produced an actual image of an astronomical object with GWs and asked what would count as one ([APS Bulletin](https://meetings.aps.org/Meeting/APR22/Session/Z01.8)). Szekerczes et al. then simulated false-color images of the Galaxy's ultracompact binaries as LISA and the AMIGO concept would see them, the closest published "what would X look like in gravitational waves" picture, with the Galaxy as the scene ([Astron. J. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/acd3f1)). Copi & Starkman's "GRADAR" maps compact objects through scattered GW "glints" ([arXiv:2201.03684](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.03684)); Takahashi, Morisaki & Suyama use pulsar GWs lensed by the Sun to probe the solar interior ([ApJ 957, 52](https://doaj.org/article/51ce68451267479c80c4d0db3914046f)); and a 2026 Physical Review Letters paper by Yan & Harms frames the Moon's normal-mode response as "GW tomography of a planetary body" ([PRL](https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/3tcp-hqf9)) [partial: title unconfirmed].

Pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) own the most developed imaging vocabulary. Boyle & Pen treat a PTA as an "imaging gravitational wave telescope" with analytic resolution and confusion limits ([arXiv:1010.4337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4337)), and Jow, Tsai & Pen count about **32 resolvable elements — 16 sky positions × 2 polarization states, roughly 58° resolution** — in a polarized PTA map ([arXiv:2507.21380](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21380v2)). Mitra et al.'s radiometry, which "points" a detector pair by a time delay and then deconvolves a direction-dependent beam, is the existing aperture-synthesis formalism ([arXiv:0708.2728](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2728)). Arrays of small detectors exist as proposals, but for the opposite purpose: Chaibi et al.'s atom-interferometer array averages down local Newtonian gravity-gradient noise by a factor of ten or more in order to see astrophysical waves ([arXiv:1601.00417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00417)), and the Levitated Sensor Detector is a genuinely compact GW detector but a single element, not an imaging array ([Aggarwal et al., PRL 128, 111101](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.111101)). The camera's signal is exactly the term that literature calls noise, which is the cleanest statement of what is new. The standing conceptual objection is in print too: GW wavelengths are usually comparable to or larger than the source, so GWs resemble sound more than sight ([arXiv:astro-ph/0210481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0210481)). Every 2023–2026 item found is astrophysical or detector technology; none images ordinary scenes.

### Real-time relativity engines render what light does, never the gravitational field

Visualization-driven relativity is an established, citable tradition. MIT Game Lab's *A Slower Speed of Light* lowers the speed of light to walking pace ([MIT Game Lab](https://gamelab.mit.edu/games/a-slower-speed-of-light/)) and its OpenRelativity toolkit was published in the American Journal of Physics ([Sherin et al., AJP 84, 369](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/483015/mod_resource/content/1/1.4938057.pdf)); James, von Tunzelmann, Franklin & Thorne turned the *Interstellar* renderer into a Classical and Quantum Gravity paper ([arXiv:1502.03808](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03808v1)); Weiskopf et al. describe the "visually enriched thought experiment" approach ([IEEE TVCG 2006 record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/NLM163796882)); and real-time black-hole renderers run on GPUs and in browsers ([Verbraeck & Eisemann](https://graphics.tudelft.nl/Publications-new/2020/VE20); [Bruneton, arXiv:2010.08735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.08735); [Reusser's WebGPU notebook](https://rreusser.github.io/notebooks/kerr-geodesics)). All of them trace light. The nearest precedents for showing the gravitational field itself are the tendex/vortex papers, which split the Weyl tensor into tidal and frame-drag parts ([Owen et al., arXiv:1012.4869](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.4869)) and include weak-field examples of rotating mass and current quadrupoles from near zone to wave zone ([Nichols et al., arXiv:1108.5486](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5486)); they are static figures, not an interactive engine or a detector-array image. The nearest interactive tools are binaryBHexp for binary black holes ([arXiv:1811.06552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.06552)) and an immersive educational GW visualization with a precomputed wave field ([iLRN 2020 PDF](https://calpoly-graphics.github.io/mixedrealitylab/files/team/iLrn_2020_An%20Immersive%20and%20Interactive%20Visualization%20of%20Gravitational%20Waves.pdf)) [partial: authors not seen]. A targeted search for an interactive GPU or WebGL tool that renders GWs or near-field tidal fields from arbitrary sources found none. On venue, Classical and Quantum Gravity fits "engine plus physics insight" and the American Journal of Physics fits "engine plus pedagogy".

### Penetrating-messenger images and fiction supply rhetoric, not prior art

The rhetorical precedent is Super-Kamiokande's neutrino image of the Sun: 500 days of data, a 90° × 90° frame, a crude picture valued because nothing can block the messenger ([NASA APOD](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap980605.html)). Muon radiography found a void at least 30 m long in Khufu's pyramid ([Morishima et al., Nature 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature24647)), and KamLAND's geoneutrino authors anticipated "a three-dimensional image of the Earth's interior" ([CERN Courier](https://cerncourier.com/a/kamland-detects-geoneutrinos/)). Fiction contains gravitational signaling and long-range gravitic detection of ships ([Weber's Honorverse notes](https://davidweber.net/posts/view/163/grav-pulse-comm-and-the-d.html/page:17)) and one "gravity lens" that maps planets with GWs ([Utah Public Radio on Crusoe's novel](https://www.upr.org/post/sci-fi-writer-predicts-major-real-life-physics-discovery)); nothing verified depicts picture-forming gravitational vision of mundane surroundings. A literature search for the project's name first hits unrelated instruments such as GravityCam, an optical lucky-imaging camera ([arXiv:1709.00244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.00244)). Exact-phrase searches for "gravitational camera", "gravitational-wave camera", "graviton camera", "gravitational vision", "gravitational-wave microscope", "gravity camera"/"mass camera" as a thought experiment, and GW "phased array" or "focal plane" returned only these collisions, the patents above and press metaphors; three attempts to search Physics StackExchange returned no pages from that site, so that channel is inconclusive rather than negative.

## Everyday scenes are near-zone tidal scenes: easy to detect, hard to resolve

### LIGO's noise budget already contains the camera's brightest subjects

The canonical calculation of what a person looks like to a GW detector is Thorne & Winstein's. Walking is the strongest routine human signal, it comes from the start and end of weight transfer between feet, and its strain noise is **√S_h ∼ 0.6×10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² (f/10 Hz)⁻⁶ [Σ(r_i/10 m)⁻⁶]¹ᐟ²** between 2.5 and 25 Hz, stated accurate to a factor of three, which is why the authors recommended keeping people about 10 m from advanced-LIGO test masses ([arXiv:gr-qc/9810016](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9810016); [Thorne's Nobel lecture](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/pdf/10.1103/RevModPhys.90.040503)). Creighton found that a 0.1 kg tumbleweed gives a signal-to-noise ratio of about 4 at 10 Hz in advanced LIGO and hundreds if it strikes a building ([arXiv:gr-qc/0007050](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0007050)); Hughes & Thorne put the seismic gravity-gradient transfer coefficient at β′ ≈ 0.35–0.6 at quiet times ([arXiv:gr-qc/9806018](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9806018)); and Saulson's 1984 paper opened the subject ([Phys. Rev. D 30, 732](https://dx.doi.org/10.1103/physrevd.30.732)). Harms et al. supply the rule that decides what a scene contains: an object moving at constant speed v past a test mass at distance r is exponentially suppressed above f ≈ v/(2πr), whether it is "a car, a person, or an atmospheric density fluctuation carried by wind" ([arXiv:1308.2074](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2074)). Harms' Living Review has a section on gravity perturbations from vibrating, rotating and moving objects, including "rules of thumb", which could not be opened ([arXiv:1507.05850](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05850)). Steady motion is therefore nearly invisible in the audio band; accelerations, impacts, vibration and rotation make the picture.

Deliberately driven laboratory masses have been "seen" this way for six decades. Sinsky & Weber detected the 1660 Hz dynamical field of an acoustically stressed bar in 1967 ([Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/4666222ce9dc99c0f54179cc161e9002)); Hirakawa, Tsubono & Oide tested the law of gravitation dynamically out to 4.2 m ([Nature 283, 184](https://www.nature.com/articles/283184a0.pdf)); and a small rotating quadrupole produced a signal 20 times the noise of the 2270 kg Explorer bar ([Astone et al. 1991](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/BF01558552)). Today kilogram-scale rotors 1.3–1.9 m from a mirror serve as absolute calibrators: the Virgo signal falls as 1/d⁴ ([arXiv:2011.03728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03728)) and reaches 0.12–0.17% amplitude uncertainty in the fourth observing run ([arXiv:2406.10028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10028); [arXiv:2511.12566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12566)); LIGO's rotor, with quadrupole and hexapole mass patterns, is predicted to better than 1% ([Ross et al., arXiv:2107.00141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00141)); and KAGRA combines the two multipoles to cancel the distance uncertainty ([Inoue et al., arXiv:1804.08249](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08249)). That last trick is one-pixel ranging by multipole ratio, a ready-made ingredient for the camera. At the extremes, the field of a 90 mg gold sphere has been measured at 12.7 mHz ([Westphal et al., Nature 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03250-7)), beam-to-beam coupling at 42 Hz ([Brack et al., arXiv:2201.03420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03420)), and the light-speed gravity signal of the 2011 Tohoku earthquake was recorded 1,000–2,000 km away before the seismic waves arrived ([Vallée et al., Science 2017](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao0746)), a channel now used for early warning ([Licciardi et al., Nature 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04672-7)).

### The radiative part of an everyday scene is about 23 orders of magnitude below the tide

The formal tools are Thorne's multipole formalism with its "local wave zone" ([Rev. Mod. Phys. 52, 299](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.52.299)) and the tidal and frame-drag tensors E_ij and B_ij with their Maxwell-like equations ([Maartens & Bassett, arXiv:gr-qc/9704059](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9704059); [Nichols et al., arXiv:1108.5486](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5486)). From these the notes derive an explicit all-zone tidal field for an oscillating mass quadrupole: E_ij is r⁻⁵ times a fourth-degree polynomial in r/ƛ, with ƛ = c/ω = λ/2π, whose r⁻⁵ term is the retarded Newtonian tide and whose r⁻¹ term is the gravitational wave [derived; believed to match Section VI of Nichols et al., which was not opened]. The transition radius ƛ is **4,770 km at 10 Hz** and 47.7 km at 1 kHz, so a scene 10 m away at 10 Hz sits at r/ƛ ≈ 2×10⁻⁶, where radiation is suppressed relative to the Newtonian tide by (r/ƛ)⁴ ≈ 2×10⁻²³ and the frame-drag field B_ij by about 10⁻⁶ [derived]. The same expansion gives a near-zone phase lag of only (r/ƛ)⁵/45 ≈ 10⁻³⁰ rad, because the terms linear and cubic in r/ƛ cancel [derived]. This is the multipole-language form of Carlip's published result that "aberration in general relativity is almost exactly canceled by velocity-dependent interactions" ([arXiv:gr-qc/9909087](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9909087)). Laboratory proposals to observe near-zone retardation exist, none has been carried out, and the notes could not verify how they handle this cancellation ([Paranjape et al., arXiv:1208.2293](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2293)). Two consequences follow for the camera: at everyday scales it images the tidal near field, not gravitational waves, and it sees the scene "instantaneously", with no possibility of ranging or time-gating by propagation delay.

Detection in this regime is absurdly easy for a LIGO-grade pixel. A strain noise of 10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² at 100 Hz is equivalent to a gradient noise of 3.9×10⁻⁹ E/√Hz [derived], nine to ten orders of magnitude below the roughly 13 E/√Hz of airborne gradiometers ([Geoscience Australia](https://www.ga.gov.au/bigobj/GA16642.pdf)) and the 20 E per point of the Birmingham tunnel survey. A 1 kg mass oscillating by 1 cm at 1 m and 10 Hz would give such a pixel a signal-to-noise ratio near 10⁸ in one second [derived from assumed round numbers]. Published per-object numbers exist only for the walking person, the tumbleweed and the calibrator rotors; estimates for vehicles, machinery, elevators or a heartbeat were not found and would be the project's own.

### Resolution improves only with the logarithm of signal-to-noise

Above its sources the Newtonian field obeys Laplace's equation, and reconstructing sources from such a field is exponentially ill-posed. Geophysics states this qualitatively: downward continuation "is unstable and will enhance noise as well as detail" ([Cooper 2004](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-stable-downward-continuation-of-potential-field-data-58gpovp0bo.pdf)). Mathematics states it rigorously: the best possible stability for the Cauchy problem for elliptic equations is logarithmic ([Alessandrini et al., arXiv:0907.2882](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2882)). The cleanest explicit formula found comes from near-field acoustic holography, attributed to Maynard, Williams & Lee: **Δx_min ≈ 27.3·D/SNR_dB** at standoff D ([J. Sound Vib. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022460X17303930)). Magnetometer imaging says the same thing in words: image quality often improves more by reducing the sensor distance than by reducing sensor noise ([Roth, Sepulveda & Wikswo 1989](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/lsp/documents/jap-roth-using-89.pdf)).

The notes show that the acoustic formula is exactly Δx = πD/ln(SNR_amplitude) and carry it over to gravity, where each spatial frequency k decays as e^(−kz) at standoff z: **Δx ≈ πz/ln(SNR)**, so that a signal-to-noise ratio of 10⁴ resolves z/3, 10⁸ resolves z/6, and even 10²⁰ resolves only z/15 [derived; no geophysics text was found that states the law in these words]. A gravitational camera's resolution is therefore a fixed fraction of the standoff distance, between roughly a third and a fifteenth across sixteen orders of magnitude in pixel sensitivity, and no lens exists because no wave propagates in the near zone. Instability is distinct from non-uniqueness: the exterior field of a static mass distribution fixes only its harmonic moments, a worry already voiced informally ([Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30454745)), and time dependence, positivity or known-density priors are what make "imaging" meaningful [derived]. Heat and stress add almost nothing to this channel: pressure, stress and thermal energy contribute about 10⁻¹² of the rest mass for laboratory bodies [derived], seven orders below the only dedicated laboratory test of active against passive gravitational mass, Kreuzer's 5×10⁻⁵ ([Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/4b7536985f9ea69579425f6a5f56ab71)), and wall stresses cancel pressure in static confined systems ([Ehlers et al., arXiv:gr-qc/0510041](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0510041)). The "moving, heavy" half of the project's scene list therefore lives in the near zone, while heat and static stress alter the near-zone picture by parts in 10¹² and are left to the radiative channel.

## Ordinary matter is nearly dark in gravitons: 10⁸ W from the Sun, 10⁻²⁹ W from a 490-tonne rotor

### The Sun is the only bright thermal source, and it delivers a few gravitons per square meter per second

Three independent calculations agree within a factor of about two on the Sun's thermal GW luminosity. Weinberg's 1965 soft-graviton estimate ([Phys. Rev. 140, B516](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRev.140.B516)) is quoted by later authors as about 6×10⁷ W; Gould's 1985 Born-approximation result is **7.9×10¹⁴ erg/s (7.9×10⁷ W)**, about a third of it from electron–electron collisions ([ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1985ApJ...288..789G/abstract)); and García-Cely & Ringwald's "Complete Gravitational-Wave Spectrum of the Sun" finds **1.3×10⁸ W** after adding photoproduction and a hydrodynamic-fluctuation term they call "previously overlooked, but essential for completing the spectrum in its low-frequency range" ([arXiv:2407.18297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18297)). Steane's recomputations add collisions with helium and Debye screening ([arXiv:2309.06972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06972); [arXiv:2403.15921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.15921)). The output is about 3×10⁻¹⁹ of the Sun's photon luminosity [derived]. Dyson put it in quanta: "about 10²⁴ gravitons per second with energy in the kilovolt range, which gives a flux at the earth of 4×10⁻⁴ gravitons per cm² per second" ([Poincaré Prize lecture](https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/poincare2012.pdf)). A recent paper puts the optical-band share at roughly 10⁵ W, about 10 gravitons per cm² per day at Earth ([arXiv:2507.17256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17256)) [partial: the originating paper was not pinned down]. That is the camera's "sunlight". García-Cely & Ringwald place the best projected helioscope sensitivity, h_c ~ 10⁻²⁹ at 10¹⁸ Hz, "still ~15 orders above the Sun spectrum".

### A gravitational Kirchhoff law exists for plasmas but was not found for ordinary matter

The fluctuation–dissipation route to thermal emission is established for hot plasmas. Ghiglieri & Laine showed that "for the largest wavelengths the emission rate is proportional to the shear viscosity of the plasma" ([arXiv:1504.02569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02569)); the absorption side is Hawking's damping rate 16πGη, quoted from secondary sources ([arXiv:1803.11397](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.11397)); and the early-universe application is the cosmic gravitational microwave background peaking near 80 GHz ([Ringwald, Schütte-Engel & Tamarit, arXiv:2011.04731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04731)). Generic thermal fields were treated by Simidzija, Kempf & Martín-Martínez, who find T⁷ scaling and conclude the emission is "so small that it is likely to never be observed" ([arXiv:1905.06988](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06988)).

Five differently phrased searches found **no paper that applies a Kirchhoff or fluctuation–dissipation emissivity to ordinary condensed matter** — liquids, solids, the Earth, laboratory bodies. The closest analogue is García-Cely & Ringwald's hydrodynamic term for the solar plasma. The notes construct the law themselves: absorption coefficient α = 16πGη/c³, volume emissivity dP/(dV dω) = 16Gηħω³/[πc⁵(e^(ħω/kT) − 1)], total P/V = 4ασT⁴ for constant η, with every body optically thin so that emission is always volume emission [derived; not confirmed against Ghiglieri & Laine's printed formula]. For water at 300 K this gives 2.3×10⁻³⁴ W/m³, about **one thermal graviton per cubic meter per 1.5 million years** [derived], and it is an upper-end figure because the zero-frequency viscosity does not apply at thermal frequencies. The research content lies in the frequency-dependent shear response: Maxwell relaxation in liquids and the lossy shear modulus μ/Q in solids replace η, and the mantle's geological viscosity cannot be inserted at gigahertz frequencies [derived]. This gap is plausible but unproven, since the generalization is a one-line consequence of known results and the literal phrases "gravitational Kirchhoff law" and "graviton emissivity" were never searched.

### Atoms, rotors and lasers radiate nothing a camera could use

Coherent and atomic sources are no brighter. The hydrogen 3d→1s single-graviton rate is 2.5×10⁻⁴⁴ s⁻¹ in Weinberg's estimate as reproduced by Rothman & Boughn ([arXiv:gr-qc/0601043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0601043)), though other sources print values four to five orders higher (see the discrepancy table) and Boughn & Rothman attribute errors in the literature to a gauge subtlety ([arXiv:gr-qc/0605052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0605052)). Halpern & Laurent treated molecules, nuclei and a stimulated-emission "gaser" in 1964 ([Nuovo Cimento](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02749891)). The textbook rotating steel beam — 20 m, 4.9×10⁵ kg, spinning at its breaking point — radiates about **2.2×10⁻²⁹ W** as restated by Chiao et al. ([arXiv:1712.08680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.08680)), a few thousand 9 Hz gravitons per second [derived]. A petawatt laser gives a strain of order 10⁻³⁷ at 10 m according to a later paper's summary of Ribeyre & Tikhonchuk ([arXiv:2108.00896](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.00896v1)), and the only proposal claiming more than 1 erg/s uses nuclear explosions ([Chapline, Nuckolls & Wood 1974](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.1064)). Electromagnetic sources have their own literature, from Gertsenshtein conversion and cavity "Hertz experiments" ([Kolosnitsyn & Rudenko, arXiv:1504.06548](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06548)) to the gravitational field of a laser pulse ([Rätzel, Wilkens & Menzel, arXiv:1511.01023](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01023)). Artificial "illumination" of a scene is out of the question; only pre-existing thermal emission is available.

"Arbitrarily sensitive pixels" is therefore a load-bearing assumption with a hard edge. The high-frequency detector literature is catalogued in the Living Reviews white paper and its 2025 update ([arXiv:2501.11723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11723)). Single-graviton detection splits by regime: stimulated absorption from highly occupied astrophysical waves is argued to be observable ([Tobar et al., Nat. Commun. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51420-8)) though it would not prove quantization ([Carney, Domcke & Rodd, arXiv:2308.12988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12988)), whereas thermal sources fall under Rothman & Boughn's verdict that "it is possible to concoct an idealized thought experiment capable of detecting one graviton; however, when anything remotely resembling realistic physics is taken into account, detection becomes impossible" ([arXiv:gr-qc/0601043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0601043)), and Dyson puts the neutrino background at 10³⁴ or more times the graviton signal for all thermal sources ([Poincaré Prize lecture](https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/poincare2012.pdf)). Even a perfect pixel faces shot noise from a few solar gravitons per square meter per second.

## Matter is transparent to gravitational waves: no shadows, mirrors or lenses below compactness of order one

### Viscous absorption is real, tiny, and capped by a compactness-type bound

Hawking's 1966 result ([INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/literature/50139)) is, in Baym, Patil & Pethick's words, that "if matter could be treated in the hydrodynamic limit the damping rate of a gravitational wave would be γ = 16πGη" ([arXiv:1707.05192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05192)). Three independent sources agree that this is the energy rate, with the amplitude falling as exp(−8πGηΔ/c³) ([Lu et al., arXiv:1803.11397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11397); [Bishop et al., arXiv:2206.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15103); [Loeb, arXiv:2001.01730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01730)); Hawking's own wording was not seen. In nearly collisionless matter the damping is "∼1/(ωτ)² smaller than the viscous result", and "damping by intergalactic or interstellar matter for all but primordial gravitational radiation can be neglected" (Baym et al.). Loeb supplies the nearest published ceiling: a gravitationally bound system "can at most dissipate a fraction ~36(σ/c)³ of the gravitational wave energy propagating through it", below 10⁻⁶ in practice. Flauger & Weinberg add that Hawking's formula applies only when the collision frequency far exceeds the wave frequency, "which it typically is not" ([arXiv:1906.04853](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04853)). From these the notes derive an absorption length ℓ = c³/(16πGη) ≈ 8×10³⁶ m for water, rewrite Loeb's bound as 12·(GM/Rc²)·(σ/c), and obtain a viscoelastic-solid formula in which the absorbed fraction over a path L is 16πGμ′L/(Qωc³), about 8×10⁻²⁰ across the Earth at 1 Hz [all derived]. No published GW absorption coefficient for a viscoelastic solid in terms of Q was found.

### Refraction is of order Gρ/ω², and elastic bodies couple only at their surfaces

Peters' 1974 abstract gives the weak-field refractive index of a medium of gravitating masses, **n − 1 = 2πGρ/ω²** ([Phys. Rev. D 9, 2207](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.9.2207)), which is about 6×10⁻¹² for Earth-density matter at 100 Hz and 10⁻¹⁴ for water at 1 kHz [derived]. Kinetic treatments add a complex index and report that transverse GWs "are not absorbed by non-collisional massive media" ([Moretti, Bombacigno & Montani, arXiv:2005.08010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08010)); the sign of n − 1 differs between treatments and the notes' reconciliation is their own reading. For solids, Dyson's 1969 abstract fixes the structural fact: "The response depends on irregularities in the shear-wave modulus, and is strongest at free surfaces" ([ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1969ApJ...156..529D/abstract)).

### Good absorbers and mirrors are argued to be impossible short of gravitational collapse

Press showed in 1979 that "a material with sufficiently large elastic shear modulus or shear viscosity will act like a gravitational conductor or 'metal'" and reflect GWs, but that such conductors must violate a condition probably satisfied by all existing matter ([Gen. Rel. Grav. 11, 105](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00756582)). Smolin's 1985 abstract goes further: "there is no material satisfying the positive energy condition which can serve as a good conductor or absorber of gravitational radiation over a finite range of frequencies" ([Gen. Rel. Grav. 17, 417](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00761902)). Garfinkle & Wald answered with a counterexample that lives only at compactness of order one, "a shell of charged matter balanced just outside its Schwarzschild radius", as described by Padmanabhan & Singh, who also note that walls thick enough to absorb all incident radiation collapse ([arXiv:gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030)); Dell disputed the box ([Gen. Rel. Grav. 19, 171](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00770328)), and an unpublished critique calls Smolin's argument a case analysis, not a proof ([W. D. Smith manuscript](https://www.rangevoting.org/WarrenSmithPages/homepage/absorber.pdf)). The claim that thin superconducting films mirror GWs ([Minter, Wegter-McNelly & Chiao, arXiv:0903.0661](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0661)) remains untested, and a conference paper proposing a test names it the "Li-Torr-Chiao conjecture" "because of the controversy surrounding it" ([AIP Conf. Proc. 1208](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/acp/article-abstract/1208/1/467/811278/Testing-the-Li-Torr-Chiao-Conjecture-A-Novel-HFGW)). The notes reconstruct Press's criterion as a shear-wave speed of order c, which makes steel "GW-metallic" only below about 10 nHz [derived; Press's own condition was not seen].

### Lensing, scattering and black-hole absorption were never researched; only derived arithmetic exists

The search budget ran out before any query on black-hole absorption cross sections, wave-optics lensing, transparent-Sun focusing, scattering cross sections, resonant-bar cross sections or "gravitational shadows" could run. What the combined notes hold at snippet level is thin: a wave-optics lensing review ([arXiv:2111.05963](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05963)), a proposal that the Galactic Center black hole boosts pulsar GW intensity toward Earth by a factor of a few thousand ([arXiv:astro-ph/0209149](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0209149v1)), and the solar-lensing paper cited earlier. Elementary lensing arithmetic in the notes gives a transparent uniform sphere a central focal length R/(6C) with C = GM/(Rc²), about 10⁴ AU for the Earth, and geometric focusing only for wavelengths below 6GM/c², which is 2.7 cm for the Earth [derived]. Summing every channel for the Earth at 1–100 Hz — viscoelastic absorption, surface conversion to seismic waves, refractive phase, lensing — the notes find no flux change above about 10⁻⁷, hence no shadow [derived].

The synthesis the project wants, a single inequality bounding the opacity, reflectivity, refractive power and focusing power of any element by a factor of order one times GM/(Rc²), was not found in print, nor was any "no shadows, no lenses" framing. Its ingredients are all standard, and two caveats are already published: narrow-band resonances escape it (Smolin's "except over a narrow band of frequencies"), and a 2026 paper reports that damping is enhanced when matter sits within a wavelength of the source ([arXiv:2605.14956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14956)), which is precisely the camera's near-zone regime.

## Spin-2 imaging mathematics exists only for far-field sky maps seen through scalar channels

### Stokes parameters, spin weights and radiometry are mature

A Stokes description of GW polarization dates to Anile & Breuer's "Gravitational Stokes parameters" of 1974 ([NASA NTRS record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740047288)). In the standard treatment the intensity I and circular polarization V are scalars on the sky while the linear combination Q ± iU is a spin-4 field ([Romano & Cornish, arXiv:1608.06889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.06889)), and the amplitude splits into gradient and curl modes with a striking detector-dependent null space: a PTA's response to curl modes is identically zero, so "half of the gravitational-wave sky will never be observed using pulsar timing, no matter how many pulsars are used" ([Gair et al., arXiv:1406.4664](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1406.4664)). Circular polarization has its own overlap functions ([Seto & Taruya, arXiv:0801.4185](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0801.4185); [Kato & Soda, arXiv:1512.09139](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1512.09139)).

Radiometry is, in structure, a far-field van Cittert–Zernike relation with one scalar output per detector. Allen & Ottewill generalized the overlap reduction function to one function per sky harmonic ([arXiv:gr-qc/9607068](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9607068)); Thrane et al. deconvolve the response by singular-value decomposition ([arXiv:0910.0858](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/0910.0858)); Renzini & Contaldi built a pixel-based maximum-likelihood map-maker ([arXiv:1806.11360](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.11360)); and Romano & Cornish print the diffraction estimate Δθ ≃ λ/2D. Floden et al. argue that the real limit comes from phase coherence and signal-to-noise, and that detected sources localize better than diffraction predicts ([arXiv:2203.17141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.17141)), a super-resolution point that should carry over to a camera. One targeted search found no paper stating an explicit van Cittert–Zernike theorem for GWs; the closest hit concerned primordial GWs blurring electromagnetic correlations ([arXiv:2312.00474](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00474)).

### Near-field treatment stops at first-order wavefront curvature

Printed resolution formulas share the form δθ ~ (1/SNR)(λ/D) and are parameter-estimation statements for a few far-zone point sources ([Boyle & Pen, arXiv:1010.4337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4337); [Cutler, arXiv:gr-qc/9703068](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9703068); [Wen & Chen, arXiv:1003.2504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2504)); Grunthal et al. derive a point-spread function for PTA maps ([arXiv:2601.13957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13957)). The only near-field work found adds first-order "Fresnel" curvature to the pulsar term in order to measure source distance ([Deng & Finn, arXiv:1008.0320](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1008.0320); [McGrath & Creighton, arXiv:2011.09561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09561); [D'Orazio & Loeb, arXiv:2009.06084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06084v1)). Nothing found treats the reactive near zone, r ≪ λ, as an imaging problem for a coherent array, and no dedicated search was run for it.

### A point pixel measuring E_ij and B_ij would be a "gravitational vector sensor" band-limited to l ≤ 4

The local invariants are in print: Maartens & Bassett construct a super-energy density and super-Poynting vector from the two tidal tensors, with the Bel–Robinson tensor as super-energy–momentum tensor ([arXiv:gr-qc/9704059](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9704059); [Senovilla, arXiv:gr-qc/9906087](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9906087)). Single-detector direction finding from the electric-type tensor alone exists: spherical antennas recover direction and polarization from five quadrupole modes ([arXiv:gr-qc/9804073](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9804073)), and the SOGRO full-tensor detector "can locate the source in the sky, modulo angle π, without necessitating triangulation" ([EPJ Web Conf.](https://www.epj-conferences.org/10.1051/epjconf/201816801005)). The electromagnetic template is Nehorai & Paldi's vector sensor, which measures all six field components and estimates direction from the Poynting vector ([IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 1994](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/vector-sensor-array-processing-for-electromagnetic-source-localiz/)). One search found no proposal to use E_ij and B_ij jointly. The notes supply it: for a plane wave the super-Poynting vector ε_abc E_bd B_cd points along the propagation direction and removes the antipodal ambiguity [derived]; and because each tensor is an l = 2 object, the second-order statistics of a point tensor pixel reach only sky multipoles **l ≤ 4** of the Stokes parameters [derived]. The repository's numerical check (`calc/single_pixel_bandlimit.py`), re-run while preparing this report, reproduces that band limit to rounding error for I, V and Q − iU, which tests the algebra and not the novelty. The nearest printed statement is Kudoh & Taruya's low-frequency result that LISA is blind to odd multipoles, seen only in a truncated excerpt ([arXiv:gr-qc/0411017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0411017)).

### The far-field formula reads as a Fourier diffraction theorem, a reading not found in print

The Fourier-domain far field of a weak-field source with arbitrary velocities is textbook material: Maggiore's Section 3.1, "Weak-field sources with arbitrary velocity" ([table of contents](https://fiteoweb.unige.ch/~maggiore/GWVol1/toc_GWVol1.pdf)), and Weinberg's equation (10.4.13) as cited in the cosmic-string literature ([arXiv:gr-qc/9405005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9405005)); the equations themselves were not seen. The notes read the formula as an emission-side Ewald sphere: at frequency ω and direction n the far field samples the transverse-traceless part of the stress tensor's spatial Fourier transform at k = ωn/c, and a source is non-radiating exactly when that part vanishes on the light cone [derived from recalled formulas]. The optical templates are Wolf's 1969 inverse-scattering solution for semi-transparent objects ([Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/35fc990eb6efedecbda7a371768c2a74)) and Devaney & Wolf's 1973 non-radiating currents ([Phys. Rev. D 8, 1044](https://www.doi.org/10.1103/PHYSREVD.8.1044)); three searches for gravitational analogues returned only electromagnetic and scalar results, and the mathematical inverse-problems literature calls the linearized Einstein inverse problem open ([Kurylev, Lassas & Uhlmann, arXiv:1406.4776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.4776v2)). Three further topics — linear GW memory from ordinary collisions, the microscopic (Irving–Kirkwood/Hardy) stress tensor as a source, and imaging templates such as the radio-interferometric measurement equation — could not be searched at all; the notes' material on them is recollection plus derivation, including the researchers' explicitly worked result that stresses carry half the radiating amplitude of a spinning rod [derived].

## Conclusion

The gravitational camera is two instruments with opposite failure modes, and that split is the most useful thing the prior work teaches. In the near zone, where every everyday scene lives, the tidal signal from moving mass is enormous by LIGO standards but harmonic, so resolution grows only with the logarithm of signal-to-noise, no lens can exist, and the scene arrives without usable delay. In the radiative channel, where heat and stress would become visible and short thermal wavelengths would permit sharp images, matter emits and absorbs so little that, by the researchers' own estimates, even a perfect pixel counts single gravitons across geological time. "Arbitrarily small and sensitive" detectors therefore buy far less than the phrase suggests, and the interesting physics of the thought experiment sits exactly where idealization stops helping. The project's strongest framing is a double inversion of existing practice: the Newtonian noise that detector arrays are built to cancel becomes the signal, and the array geometry flips from kiloparsec baselines viewing far-zone point sources to sub-wavelength pixels viewing near-zone extended ones.

The main risk to the novelty claims is not a rival camera but a sentence in an unread classic. Each derived result is a short consequence of standard formulas, which is why it may be new as a statement and also why it may already sit in Smolin, Press, Weinberg, Nichols et al. or García-Cely & Ringwald. Reading roughly fifteen full texts and running about ten exact-phrase database queries is small work against that risk, and the two blocks of physics that were never searched — lensing, scattering and absorption by compact objects, and memory, microscopic stress and coherence-theory templates — should get a second research pass before the paper's related-work section is written.
