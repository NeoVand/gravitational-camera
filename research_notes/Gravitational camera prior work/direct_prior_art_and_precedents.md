# Direct prior art and conceptual precedents for a "gravitational camera"

Research date: 2026-11-15. Method and limits (read first): 52 web searches were executed; 8 further planned searches were refused because the session's shared web-search budget ran out. Direct page fetches were blocked (arxiv.org and patents.justia.com are outside the session's network allowlist), so NO source was opened in full text. Every item below was therefore verified only at the level of "title + URL + abstract/snippet seen in search results". Verification tags used throughout:

- **[SR]** = title, URL and the stated content were seen in this session's search results (full text not opened).
- **[SR-partial]** = URL/title seen, but one or more bibliographic details (named in the entry) were not confirmed.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** = from the researcher's background knowledge only; nothing seen in this session supports it. Check before citing.

"Negative result" below always means "this web-search tool returned nothing relevant for this phrase", not "arXiv/ADS/INSPIRE/Google Scholar full-text search returned nothing" (those databases could not be queried directly).

## 1. Has anyone proposed, named or analyzed a "gravitational camera" / gravitational-wave imaging of ordinary objects / a pixel array of miniature GW detectors?

### Takeaway
No paper, essay or patent was found that proposes or analyzes an imaging array of idealized point-like gravitational-wave/tidal-strain detectors used to picture everyday scenes. The nearest things that do exist are (i) a US patent that literally uses the words "gravity camera" for a scaled-up array of static gravimeters, (ii) a 2004 patent application and conference papers on "gravitational wave imaging"/surveillance with generated high-frequency GWs (rejected by a 2008 JASON study), (iii) a 2005 theoretical paper on all-gravitational-wave holography, and (iv) astrophysical "GW imaging" framing work from NASA Goddard (2022-2023).

### Cited Findings

**A. Closest direct prior art (same words or same idea, different physics or regime)**

- **US Patent 10,983,244 B2, "Method for scanning an object using a gravimeter" [SR-partial: inventor, assignee, dates not confirmed].** The patent family describes a scaled-up array of its (torsion-balance) detectors as a way to build a "gravity camera"; claimed use is imaging gravimetric density profiles of non-trivial mass distributions (e.g. tumours in situ); it states that a sensitive gravimetric camera could in principle "see" a person walking around through hundreds of metres of concrete; ~1 g at 10 cm is about the limit of the described torsion-balance design. This is quasi-static Newtonian mass-density imaging, not gravitational-wave/strain imaging, and it is a patent claim of possibility, not demonstrated hardware. This is the single item a referee is most likely to hold up against the name "gravitational camera". — [Google Patents US10983244](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10983244); [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10983244)

### Inferences
- The idea space splits into four occupied corners, none of which is the proposed project: (1) static Newtonian "gravity cameras" (patent US 10,983,244; MEMS "multi-pixel gravity imagers"; gravity cartography) — real, but DC mass-density imaging; (2) active HFGW transmission imaging (Baker) — discredited as engineering; (3) all-GW holography formalism (Bar) — no detectors, no scenes; (4) astrophysical "GW imaging" (Kelly et al.; Szekerczes et al.) — sky maps of binaries.
- The physics that an honest "gravitational camera of everyday scenes" must confront is already in Thorne & Winstein and the Newtonian-calibrator papers: at human distances the signal an ideal strain pixel records from a person, car or rotor is overwhelmingly the time-varying near-zone (Newtonian, 1/r^3 tidal) field, not radiation. A referee will ask the paper to state clearly whether the camera images the tidal near field, the radiative field, or both, and how that differs from a gravity-gradiometer array.
- Because detectors much smaller than the wavelength are assumed, the device is a near-field/aperture-synthesis imager rather than a focusing camera; the "GWs are more like sound than sight" argument (astro-ph/0210481) and the static-inverse-problem non-uniqueness worry are the predictable conceptual objections.
- Any use of "arbitrarily sensitive" pixels invites the graviton-detectability literature (Rothman & Boughn; Dyson; Tobar et al.) and the JASON conclusion; framing the work explicitly as an idealized thought experiment plus visualization, not a feasibility claim, avoids the Baker/HFGW association.

## 2. Closest astrophysical analogs: PTAs as imaging telescopes, high-angular-resolution GW astronomy, sky mapping/radiometry, arrays of small GW detectors

### Takeaway
"Imaging" language is well established for pulsar timing arrays and for stochastic-background radiometry, and arrays of small or compact GW detectors (atom-interferometer arrays, levitated sensors, cavities) are actively proposed, but in every case the array is used for noise rejection or to localize/map astrophysical sources on the sky. None treats each detector as a pixel of a focal plane or images nearby ordinary matter.

### Cited Findings
- **L. Boyle & U.-L. Pen, "Pulsar timing arrays as imaging gravitational wave telescopes: angular resolution and source (de)confusion", arXiv:1010.4337 (submitted Oct 2010, revised Mar 2013) [SR; journal reference Phys. Rev. D 86, 124028 (2012) is from background knowledge — UNVERIFIED].** Treats a PTA as a telescope for GW point sources (supermassive black-hole binaries); derives analytic angular resolution and confusion limits; argues the rule "confused above about one source per frequency bin" is too pessimistic for PTAs; a pulsar with accurately known distance acts like one baseline of a diffraction-limited interferometer; with poorly known distances sources are still localized "for reasons that have less to do with diffraction, and more to do with topology". Difference from the camera: the "pixels" are kiloparsec-scale Earth-pulsar baselines, sources are nanohertz binaries, the image is a sky map. — [arXiv:1010.4337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4337)
- **Jow, Tsai & Pen, "How many pixels are there in a polarized pulsar timing array map?", arXiv:2507.21380; Phys. Rev. D 113, 043034 (17 Feb 2026) [SR].** Finds about N_res = 16 x 2 = 32 resolvable elements (16 sky positions x 2 polarization states), i.e. ~58 degree resolution, reachable with more than ~20 pulsars; without pulsar distances adding pulsars only improves signal-to-noise, not resolving power. The most recent and most directly "pixel-counting" analog (2026). — [arXiv:2507.21380](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21380v2)
- **Tsai, Jow & Pen, "Reaching diffraction-limited localization with coherent PTAs", arXiv:2512.10795 [SR in one search; a second search failed to re-find it].** Coherent method using precise pulsar distances to approach diffraction-limited resolution for individual sources. — [arXiv:2512.10795](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10795)
- **"Gravitational-Wave Sky Mapping with Pulsar Timing Arrays: The Full Earth-Pulsar Response and Fundamental Resolution Limits", arXiv:2607.19329 [SR-partial: title and URL only; authors and content not seen].** — [arXiv:2607.19329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19329)
- **Curyło, Thrane, Lasky & Gaynor, "A comprehensive framework for phase-coherent mapping of the gravitational-wave sky with pulsar timing arrays", arXiv:2604.19073 (2026) [SR].** Preserves amplitude, phase and polarisation of the signal in every sky pixel. — [arXiv:2604.19073](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19073)
- 2026 review "Pulsar timing arrays: the emerging gravitational-wave landscape", arXiv:2603.13643: number of resolvable continuous-wave sources scales linearly with pulsar count [SR]. — [arXiv:2603.13643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13643)
- **J. Baker, T. Baker et al., "High angular resolution gravitational wave astronomy", arXiv:1908.11410 (ESA Voyage 2050 white paper); Experimental Astronomy 51, 1441-1470 (2021) [SR].** Current/near-future detectors localize to tens-hundreds of square degrees; argues for an observatory with arcminute precision or better as a large-baseline space interferometer array (L-class), enabling standard-siren cosmology, host identification, background anisotropy. Difference: long-baseline astrometry of astrophysical point sources; no focal plane, no near-field scenes. — [arXiv:1908.11410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.11410); [NTRS record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024023)
- **Mitra, Dhurandhar, Souradeep, Lazzarini, Mandic, Bose & Ballmer, "Gravitational wave radiometry: Mapping a stochastic gravitational wave background", Phys. Rev. D 77, 042002 (2008), arXiv:0708.2728 [SR].** Cross-correlates two detectors with a time delay that "points" the baseline at a sky direction; the raw map is the true sky convolved with a direction-dependent beam and must be deconvolved (CMB-map-making analogy). Extension: Talukder, Mitra & Bose, "Multibaseline gravitational wave radiometry" (2011); fast map-making with folded data, arXiv:1803.08285. D 93, 021101(R) (2016), arXiv:1601.00417 [SR].** A correlated array of atom interferometers; the array is used to average down Newtonian (gravity-gradient) noise by a factor of ten or more, targeting 0.3-3 Hz with peak strain sensitivity 3e-23/sqrt(Hz) at 2 Hz. Difference: the array rejects the local gravity field (exactly the signal a gravitational camera would keep) to see astrophysical GWs. Related: ELGAR, arXiv:2007.04014; Geiger review arXiv:1611.09911. — [arXiv:1601.00417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00417); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016PhRvD..93b1101C); [ELGAR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.04014); [arXiv:1611.09911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.09911)

A genuinely small GW detector, but single-element, not an imaging array. — [PRL accepted](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.111101)

### Inferences
- The term "imaging" in GW astronomy currently means angular localization or sky-map synthesis from widely separated baselines; "pixel" means a sky pixel, not a detector element. A pixel array of co-located miniature detectors forming an image of a nearby scene inverts the usual geometry (detector spacing far below a wavelength; sources in the near zone).
- Existing arrays of small detectors are designed to cancel local gravity-gradient "Newtonian noise"; the gravitational camera's signal is, from the standpoint of that literature, exactly the noise term. This is a clean way to state what is new.
- The radiometer/map-making mathematics (beam, deconvolution, number of resolvable elements) provides ready-made formal tools and the natural citations for an "image formation" section.

## 3. Visualization and game-engine precedents

## 4. "See through everything" imaging analogs: neutrino image of the Sun, muon tomography, geoneutrinos

### Takeaway
The canonical precedents for imaging with a penetrating messenger are well documented: Super-Kamiokande's neutrino image of the Sun (500-day exposure, taken through the Earth when the Sun is below the horizon), cosmic-ray muon radiography from Alvarez (1970) to the Khufu "big void" (2017), and geoneutrino detection (KamLAND 2005) with proposed directional "tomography" of the Earth.

### Cited Findings
- **Super-Kamiokande neutrino image of the Sun [SR].** NASA APOD 5 June 1998, "Neutrinos in the Sun": 500 days of data were needed to produce the "neutrino image"; the frame covers 90 x 90 degrees centred on the Sun, brighter colour = larger neutrino flux; credit R. Svoboda and K. Gordan (LSU). — [APOD](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap980605.html); [HEASARC picture page](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/objects/heapow/archive/solar_system/superk_sun.html)
- The "seen through the Earth" framing: "The Sun seen through the Earth in 'neutrino light'" (gives 503 days exposure and a 50,000-ton detector 1 km underground; HEASARC gives 55,000 tons — minor source disagreement) [SR, secondary]. — [Strange Paths](https://strangepaths.com/the-sun-seen-through-the-earth-in-neutrino-light/2007/01/06/en/); [later essay](https://www.stevestewartwilliams.com/p/peering-at-the-sun-through-the-earth)
- **L. W. Alvarez et al., "Search for hidden chambers in the pyramids", Science 167, 832-839 (1970) [SR via reference lists].** Spark chambers in Khafre's pyramid; no unknown chamber found. — [Morishima et al. reference list, arXiv:1711.01576](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1711.01576)
- **K. Morishima et al., "Discovery of a big void in Khufu's Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons", Nature (2017), DOI 10.1038/nature24647, arXiv:1711.01576 [SR].** Void at least 30 m long above the Grand Gallery, seen with three independent detector technologies (nuclear emulsion, scintillator hodoscopes, gas detectors). Follow-up: Procureur, Morishima et al., Nat. Commun. (2023), corridor-shaped structure. — [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature24647); [APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2023/05/muon-telescope-giza-pyramid)
- **K. N. Borozdin et al., "Surveillance: Radiographic imaging with cosmic-ray muons", Nature 422, 277 (2003) [SR via reference list only; content not seen].** — [arXiv:1711.01576 reference list](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1711.01576)
- **T. Araki et al. (KamLAND), "Experimental investigation of geologically produced antineutrinos with KamLAND", Nature 436 (2005) [SR].** First geoneutrino detection (about 25 events after background subtraction per CERN Courier; other secondary sources say ~28 — check the paper); the authors noted that with geoneutrinos "it should be possible to build up a three-dimensional image of the Earth's interior". — [paper PDF copy](https://www.geol.umd.edu/%7Emcdonoug/KITP%20Website%20for%20Bill/papers/Measurements/Araki_etal_(Nature_05).pdf); [CERN Courier](https://cerncourier.com/a/kamland-detects-geoneutrinos/)

Review: "Geo-neutrinos and Earth's interior", arXiv:0707.3203 [SR, title]. — [hep-ph/0406001](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0406001); [arXiv:0707.3203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.3203)
- 2026 preprint proposing an Ocean Bottom Detector (kiloton liquid scintillator on the sea floor) for directional geoneutrino "planetary tomography" of large-scale mantle structures [SR, news page of preprint]. — [astrobiology.com summary](https://astrobiology.com/2026/06/planetary-tomography-towards-imaging-earths-large-scale-structures-by-directional-geoneutrino-detection-with-ocean-bottom-detector.html)
- Nature news feature on geoneutrino researchers wanting multiple detectors around the Earth to do "a sort of tomography on the mantle" [SR; date unresolved — search summary said 2024, the URL volume number suggests 2013]. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/496017a)

### Inferences
- The neutrino picture of the Sun is the best rhetorical precedent: a crude, low-resolution image (tens of degrees, limited by scattering kinematics) made with a messenger that passes through the whole Earth, valued precisely because nothing can block it. Gravity is the only messenger that is even more penetrating (it cannot be shielded at all), which is the framing hook.
- Muon and gravity methods are already linked in practice (both are used for density imaging of volcanoes, tunnels and cargo), so the gravity-gradiometry items in Section 1 belong in the same "see-through imaging" lineage.

## 6. Novelty verdict

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, the specific object — a focal-plane-like array of idealized point GW/tidal-strain detectors forming images of everyday dynamic scenes, analyzed as physics and realized as a real-time simulator — appears unclaimed; the phrase "gravity camera" and the notion of "multi-pixel gravity imaging" are not unclaimed, and the verdict carries the caveat that literature databases could not be queried directly.

### Cited Findings
- Appears unclaimed (nothing found in 52 searches): a physics treatment of image formation by an array of sub-wavelength GW/strain detectors viewing near-zone, human-scale, time-dependent sources; a catalogue of what moving, heavy, hot or stressed everyday objects look like in the strain/tidal channel; an interactive engine rendering such images. Closest search outcome for the engine — [binaryBHexp](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.06552); for the concept — [APS 2022 "Imaging through Gravitational Waves"](https://meetings.aps.org/Meeting/APR22/Session/Z01.8)
- Already claimed, must be cited and distinguished: "gravity camera" as an array of gravimeters imaging mass through concrete — [US 10,983,244](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10983244); "multi-pixel gravity imager" — [NEWTON-g/Wee-g](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2020/EGU2020-1244.html); "gravitational wave imaging" of objects with generated HFGWs — [US 2004/0056545](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20040056545); GW holography — [Bar](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0509052)
- Nearest work a referee would point to, in likely order: (1) Thorne & Winstein on what a human looks like to LIGO — [gr-qc/9810016](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9810016); (2) LIGO Newtonian calibrator, a rotor seen by LIGO — [arXiv:2107.00141](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00141); (3) gravity-gradiometer imaging — [Stray et al.](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/quantum-sensing-for-gravity-cartography) and [Kirkendall et al.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4215058/similar); (4) the JASON rejection of HFGW imaging — [FAS](https://fas.org/blogs/secrecy/2008/12/jason_study); (5) PTA imaging and pixel counting — [Boyle & Pen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4337), [Jow, Tsai & Pen](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21380v2); (6) radiometer map-making — [Mitra et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.2728); (7) tendex/vortex visualization incl. weak-field rotating quadrupoles — [Nichols et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5486); (8) graviton-detection limits — [Rothman & Boughn](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0601043), [Tobar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15440); (9) "GWs are sound not sight" — [astro-ph/0210481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0210481); (10) Sinsky & Weber's stressed-matter source — [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/4666222ce9dc99c0f54179cc161e9002)

### Inferences
- A defensible novelty statement: "Static gravity imaging with sensor arrays, astrophysical GW sky-mapping, and visualizations of tidal fields each exist separately; to our knowledge no one has analyzed, or simulated in real time, the image that an idealized array of local strain/tidal detectors would form of ordinary dynamic scenes, including the transition from the near-zone tidal field to the radiative field." The words "to our knowledge" should stay until an ADS/INSPIRE full-text check is done.
- The project should not claim the names "gravity camera" or "gravity imager" as new; "gravitational camera" with an explicit definition (dynamic strain-tensor imaging, every pixel a GW detector) differentiates it, but the paper should cite US 10,983,244 and the Glasgow/NEWTON-g usage up front.
