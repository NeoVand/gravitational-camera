## 1. Damping of GWs in viscous media (Hawking 16πGη; amplitude vs energy; collisionless regime; absorption lengths)

### Takeaway
The hydrodynamic (ωτ ≪ 1) result is an **energy** e-folding rate Γ_E = 16πGη/c² (amplitude rate 8πGη/c²), frequency-independent and sensitive only to shear viscosity; in the collisionless regime (ωτ ≫ 1) it is suppressed by ~1/(ωτ)², so real matter between sources and us is utterly transparent. Loeb (2020) converts this into the closest published "compactness-type" bound: a bound system dissipates at most ~36(σ/c)³ ≡ 12·(GM/Rc²)·(σ/c) of the GW energy crossing it.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Hawking's paper: S. W. Hawking, "Perturbations of an expanding universe", ApJ 145, 544–554 (1966), DOI 10.1086/148793 — [INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/literature/50139). His PhD-thesis abstract states that "the propagation and absorption of gravitational radiation is also investigated in this approximation" — [Cambridge repository, "Properties of expanding universes"](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/251038).
- [V] Baym, Patil & Pethick, "Damping of gravitational waves by matter", PRD 96, 084033 (2017), arXiv:1707.05192. Introduction: "Half a century ago, Hawking showed that if matter could be treated in the hydrodynamic limit the damping rate of a gravitational wave would be γ = 16πGη", G = Newton's constant, η = (shear) viscosity (c = 1 there). — [arXiv:1707.05192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05192)
- [V] Baym et al. abstract-level results: (i) two physically distinct mechanisms, collisional and Landau damping; (ii) collisions generate shear viscosity but also erase anisotropic stresses, which suppresses the damping (paraphrase); (iii) "Maximal collisional damping of a gravitational wave, independent of the details of the matter collisions, is significant only when its wavelength is comparable to the size of the horizon", so "damping by intergalactic or interstellar matter for all but primordial gravitational radiation can be neglected"; (iv) the maximum damping of the amplitude of a wave of frequency ω is of order 1/(ωτ_U), τ_U = age of the universe; (v) they generalize Weinberg's calculation to include collisions and massive particles and interpret the collisionless limit as Landau damping, which cannot occur in a static flat spacetime but is enabled by cosmic expansion (paraphrase). — [arXiv:1707.05192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05192); [accepted manuscript, APS](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevD.96.084033)
- [V] Collisionless regime: in nearly collisionless matter "the damping is ∼1/(ωτ)² smaller than the viscous result" (τ = collision time). — [arXiv:1707.05192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05192). Same statement in Loeb's abstract: "At high wave frequencies, the dissipation is further suppressed by the square of the ratio between the collision frequency and the wave frequency." — [arXiv:2001.01730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01730)
- [V] **Amplitude-vs-energy convention (three independent sources agree that 16πGη is the energy rate):**
  - Lu, et al. 2018 model the strain with the suppression factor e^(−βD/2), β ≡ 16πGη, D = distance; β "only depends on the shear viscosity of the fluid, rather than the bulk viscosity or the GW frequency". So amplitude ∝ exp(−8πGηD) (c = 1). — [arXiv:1803.11397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11397)
  - Bishop, van der Walt & Naidoo write the amplitude law in SI: H(r+Δ) = H(r)·exp(−8πGηΔ/c³), H = magnitude of the metric perturbation, Δ = path length in the viscous fluid. — [arXiv:2206.15103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15103)
  - Loeb writes the (energy) dissipation time t_diss = c²/(16πGη) [his Eq. (1)], η = dynamic viscosity. — [arXiv:2001.01730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01730)
  - Giovannini quotes the Hawking rate as Γ = 2ℓ_P²η (search extract: with ℓ_P² = 8πG, i.e. 16πGη). — [arXiv:2111.13544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13544)
- [V] Loeb, "Upper Limit on the Dissipation of Gravitational Waves in Gravitationally Bound Systems", arXiv:2001.01730 (published in ApJL). Chain of formulas: η ∼ ρλσ [Eq. (2)], λ = 1/(nA) = collision mean free path, n = ρ/m, A = collision cross-section, σ = 1-D velocity dispersion; viscosity is maximal for λ ∼ R (system size), η ∼ ρRσ, giving "an upper limit on the dissipated fraction of the GW energy, ε_diss < (16π)GρR²σ/c³". Abstract: a bound system "can at most dissipate a fraction ~36(sigma/c)^3 of the gravitational wave energy propagating through it", even if its dynamical time is shorter than the wave period (paraphrase); the limit is saturated for particles with mean free path equal to the system size (hot protons in galaxy clusters, strongly self-interacting dark matter in halos); "the dissipated fraction, <10⁻⁶, does not degrade the use of gravitational waves as cosmological probes"; EM counterparts of the dissipation are too faint to detect. — [arXiv:2001.01730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01730)
- [V] Flauger & Weinberg, "Gravitational waves in cold dark matter", PRD 97, 123506 (2018), arXiv:1801.00386: effects are suppressed either because CDM is highly non-relativistic or because it is a small fraction of the energy density; "the dominant effect on astrophysical waves is a small frequency dependent modification of the propagation speed". APS synopsis: a wave with wavelength the size of the Universe would be slowed by roughly one part per million. — [arXiv:1801.00386](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00386); [APS Physics synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v11/s66)
- [V] Flauger & Weinberg, "Absorption of gravitational waves from distant sources", PRD 99, 123030 (2019), arXiv:1906.04853: absorption by inverse bremsstrahlung (during collisions in intervening matter) has a rate that "increases with decreasing frequency ν as ν⁻³", but is "almost entirely cancelled by stimulated emission" and is no obstacle to pulsar timing; Hawking's result applies only when the collision frequency is much higher than the wave frequency, "which it typically is not". — [arXiv:1906.04853](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04853); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1530005-absorption-gravitational-waves-from-distant-sources)
- [V] Goswami, Chakravarty, Mohanty & Prasanna, PRD 95, 103509 (2017), arXiv:1603.02635: GWs propagate through ideal fluids without dispersion or dissipation; with non-zero shear viscosity they are dissipated at a rate proportional to the viscosity; GW150914 (410 Mpc) gives cosmological shear viscosity < 10⁹ Pa·s. — [arXiv:1603.02635](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02635); [conference abstract](https://indico.cern.ch/event/452998/contributions/2148598/)
- [V] Observational limits on β ≡ 16πGη: β < 6.75×10⁻⁴ Mpc⁻¹ (95% CL, combined GW data) — [Lu et al., arXiv:1803.11397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11397); forecast Δβ = 10⁻⁶ Mpc⁻¹ for Einstein Telescope with 10 strongly lensed events — [Cao et al., arXiv:2012.12462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12462).

### Inferences
- [D] **Convention resolution.** Energy rate Γ_E = 16πGη/c² [s⁻¹]; amplitude rate Γ_A = 8πGη/c². Absorption lengths: ℓ_E = c³/(16πGη) = 8.03×10³³ m × (1 Pa·s/η); ℓ_A = 2ℓ_E. The task's phrase "16πGη" is the **energy** (intensity) rate; no source found uses it as the amplitude rate.
- [D] Consistency checks: β < 6.75×10⁻⁴ Mpc⁻¹ ⇒ η < 1.8×10⁸ Pa·s; "βD ≲ 1 over 410 Mpc" ⇒ η ≲ 6×10⁸ Pa·s, matching the quoted "<10⁹ Pa·s".
- [D] **Ordinary materials (hydrodynamic formula taken at face value):** water (η = 10⁻³ Pa·s): ℓ_E ≈ 8×10³⁶ m ≈ 8.5×10²⁰ ly. One absorption length across the Earth's diameter (1.27×10⁷ m) would need η ≈ 6×10²⁶ Pa·s; across the Sun's diameter η ≈ 6×10²⁴ Pa·s; across a 20 km neutron star η ≈ 4×10²⁹ Pa·s (4×10³⁰ poise). These are required values, not measured ones.
- [D] **Loeb's bound is a compactness bound.** With ρ = 3M/(4πR³): ε_diss < 16πGρR²σ/c³ = 12·(GM/Rc²)·(σ/c). Using the virial relation GM/R ≈ 3σ² reproduces the published prefactor: 36(σ/c)³ ≈ 6.9·(GM/Rc²)^{3/2}. So for self-gravitating matter, opacity ≲ 12·C·(σ/c) with C ≡ GM/(Rc²). For the Sun (σ ≈ 2.5×10⁵ m/s) the ceiling is ≈ 2×10⁻⁸ (and the real value is far lower because the mean free path ≪ R_⊙); for a neutron star (C ≈ 0.2) the bound is not restrictive (order unity).
- [D] **Frequency-dependent viscosity / maximum damping.** A relaxation-time model η(ω) = η₀/(1+ω²τ²) with η₀ ∼ ρv²τ reproduces the [V] 1/(ωτ)² suppression; it is maximal at ωτ ≈ 1, η_max ∼ ρv²/(2ω), giving Γ_E/ω ≲ 8πGρv²/(ω²c²) = 2(ω_J/ω)²(v/c)², ω_J² ≡ 4πGρ. Per-cycle damping is therefore bounded by (Jeans frequency / wave frequency)² × (v/c)², which is the content of Baym et al.'s "significant only at horizon-scale wavelengths".
- [D] **Viscoelastic solid (answer to "is there a Q-formula?").** Writing the TT stress as σ_ij = −μ*(ω)h_ij with complex shear modulus μ* = μ′ − iμ″ (μ″ = ωη for a Newtonian fluid, μ″ = μ′/Q for a solid), the linearized wave equation gives ω² = c²k² + (16πG/c²)μ*, hence Γ_E = 16πGμ″/(ωc²) and absorbed fraction over a path L: f = 16πGμ′L/(Qωc³). This reduces to Hawking's rate for μ″ = ωη. Example: mantle-like μ′ = 10¹¹ Pa, Q = 300, 1 Hz ⇒ η_eff = 5×10⁷ Pa·s, f ≈ 8×10⁻²⁰ across the Earth's diameter (∝ 1/ω). Valid only for bodies large compared with the shear wavelength v_s/ω (otherwise free surfaces relieve the strain — Dyson's point, §2). Plugging the mantle's *geological* viscosity (~10²¹ Pa·s) into Hawking's formula is wrong by >10 orders of magnitude because ωτ_Maxwell ≫ 1.

## 2. GWs in elastic solids (Dyson 1969; bars; lunar/elastic-body detectors; viscoelastic absorption)

### Takeaway
Dyson's 1969 abstract confirms the key structural fact: an elastic solid responds to a GW only through irregularities (gradients, discontinuities) of the shear modulus, most strongly at free surfaces. I found no published closed-form GW absorption coefficient for a viscoelastic solid in terms of loss modulus or Q in this session (a one-line derivation is given in §1 Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- [V] F. J. Dyson, "Seismic response of the Earth to a gravitational wave in the 1-Hz band", ApJ 156, 529 (1969). Abstract: linearized-GR treatment of an elastic solid, first a stationary flat Earth then a spherical rotating Earth; "The response depends on irregularities in the shear-wave modulus, and is strongest at free surfaces"; on a rotating Earth the response splits into five frequencies ω, ω ± Ω, ω ± 2Ω; pulsar signals are estimated to lie below seismic noise (the power of ten is garbled in the OCR extract) but detection is "not hopeless". The body discusses absorption at the core–mantle interface. — [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1969ApJ...156..529D/abstract); [ADS scan](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1969ApJ...156..529D)
- [V] Later work describes Dyson's contribution as the amplitude of seismic waves produced by GWs incident on a free, flat surface. — ["Constraining the gravitational wave energy density of the Universe using Earth's ring", arXiv:1406.1147](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.1147)
- [V] Modern elastic-body response literature surfaced (titles only): "The response of the Moon to gravitational waves" — [arXiv:2403.05118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05118); "Thick Lunar Crust Amplifies Deci-Hertz Gravitational-Wave Signal" (2026) — [arXiv:2601.16567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16567); "Physical effects of gravitational waves: pedagogical examples" — [arXiv:2211.16955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16955); EPSC2024-811 abstract — [Copernicus](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EPSC2024/EPSC2024-811.html).
- [V] W. H. Press (1979) treats elastic shear modulus and shear viscosity on the same footing as the GW "conductivity" of a material (see §4). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00756582)

### Inferences
- [D] Why only ∇μ couples: the GW force density on the lattice is ∂_j(μh_ij), which vanishes for uniform μ because h_ij is transverse-traceless; elastic waves are launched only where μ varies (free surface: μ drops to zero). The bulk lattice is nevertheless physically strained by h/2, which is what dissipates (formula in §1).
- [D] Free-surface conversion efficiency by dimensional analysis: GW flux c³ω²h²/(32πG) versus shear-wave flux ~½ρv_s(ωξ)² with ξ ~ h·v_s/ω gives efficiency ~ 16πGρv_s³/(ω²c³) × (angular factor) = (ω_J/ω)²·(v_s/c)³·O(1). For crustal rock (ρ = 3000 kg/m³, v_s = 3.5 km/s) at 1 Hz this is ~10⁻²².
- [D] For a resonant bar, peak absorbed fraction ≈ (integrated cross-section × 2Q/πν₀)/area scales as Q × (GM/Lc²) × (v_s/c): again compactness × (v_s/c), boosted by Q only inside a bandwidth ν₀/Q (uses the [U] bar formula below).

## 3. Refractive index of matter for GWs

### Takeaway
Peters (1974) gives n − 1 = 2πGρ/ω² for GWs in a medium of gravitating masses; this is ~10⁻¹¹ for rock at 100 Hz and ~10⁻¹⁴ for water at 1 kHz, i.e. n − 1 = ½(ω_Jeans/ω)². Kinetic-theory treatments (Polnarev, Chesters, Flauger–Weinberg, Moretti et al.) add a complex, velocity-dependent index and report no Landau damping of tensor modes in flat space; the sign of n − 1 differs between treatments.

### Cited Findings
- [V] P. C. Peters, "Index of refraction for scalar, electromagnetic, and gravitational waves in weak gravitational fields", PRD 9, 2207–2218 (15 April 1974). Abstract: solves scattering of scalar, EM and gravitational waves by the gravitational field of a single particle (weak field, small amplitude; plane and spherical incident waves), then sums over a medium: for gravitational waves "the index of refraction n is given by n − 1 = 2πGρ/ω², assuming n − 1 is small, and for electromagnetic waves n = 1 to the same order"; the scalar result depends on the wave equation used; δ-function wave packets show consistency with causality "even though, in some cases, the group velocity exceeds the velocity of light". ρ = mass density of the medium, ω = angular frequency. — [APS](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.9.2207); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/4284872)
- [V] P. Szekeres, "Linearized gravitation theory in macroscopic media", Annals of Physics **64**, 599–630 (1971) (the brief says vol. 62; citing papers give 64). Linearized gravity on Minkowski background with a linearized Bianchi identity treated like Maxwell's equations and Kaufman's molecular-moment method; defines the quadrupole polarization tensor of a "molecule" and a constitutive relation for the induced quadrupole; such polarization can produce dispersion or damping of GWs. — reference list of [arXiv:1712.08680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.08680); descriptions in [gr-qc/0307077](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0307077) and [arXiv:2111.11342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11342)
- [V] Critiques of Szekeres: he perturbed the molecule with only part of the Weyl tensor, kept only the induced (not static) quadrupole, and the model yields four transverse degrees of freedom — [arXiv:1805.08018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08018); in the strong-polarization case the potential is confined within the source and decays exponentially outside, with no Newtonian limit — [gr-qc/0307077](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0307077).
- [V] "Polnarev, then Chesters used methods of plasma kinetic theory for a GW in hot collisionless gas and derived the complex index of refraction for GW"; in a collisionless gas, damping of GWs analogous to Landau damping is absent. Chesters: PRD 7, 2863–2868 (1973). — [Barta & Vasúth, arXiv:1708.05576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05576)
- [V] Moretti, Bombacigno & Montani, "Gravitational Landau damping for massive scalar modes" (EPJC 2020): "It is well established that transverse gravitational waves are not absorbed by non-collisional massive media"; only subluminal (massive scalar) modes can be Landau damped. — [arXiv:2005.08010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08010). Related: dielectric representation of the "gravitational plasma" — [arXiv:2203.16990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16990); tensor Landau damping only with parity-violating birefringence — [arXiv:2210.07673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07673); "Landau damping is strictly forbidden for isotropic configurations and static flat spacetimes, where gravitational modes have a superluminal phase velocity in the medium" — [arXiv:2507.23069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23069).
- [V] Flauger & Weinberg: small frequency-dependent change of GW propagation speed in cold dark matter (≈10⁻⁶ at Hubble-scale wavelength, described as a slowing). — [APS synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v11/s66)
- [V] Other titles surfaced: Garg & Dodin, "Gravitational wave modes in matter" (modes strongly coupled to matter have large refractive index N) — [arXiv:2204.09095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09095); "Modified gravity as a diagravitational medium" (cites Peters for a scalar effective index) — [arXiv:1805.09629](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09629); "Effect of a low density dust shell on the propagation of gravitational waves" — [arXiv:1912.08289](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08289); "The gravito-optic effect" — [arXiv:2504.21225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21225).

### Inferences
- [D] Magnitudes of Peters' index, n − 1 = 2πGρ/ω² = ½(ω_J/ω)² with ω_J² = 4πGρ: Earth mean density (5515 kg/m³): 5.9×10⁻⁸ at 1 Hz, 5.9×10⁻¹² at 100 Hz; water at 1 kHz: 1.1×10⁻¹⁴; lead at 1 kHz: 1.2×10⁻¹³. The formula ceases to be small only at nuclear density (ρ = 5×10¹⁷ kg/m³ at 1 kHz gives ≈ 5), i.e. only for compact objects.
- [D] Accumulated refractive phase across a uniform sphere's diameter: Δφ = (n − 1)ω·2R/c = 3·(GM/Rc²)·(λ/2πR). Earth at 100 Hz: 1.6×10⁻¹⁰ rad. So refractive phase ≲ compactness whenever λ ≲ R; it is a (λ/R)² correction to the ordinary Shapiro/lensing phase ~4C·(ωR/c).
- [D] Sign conventions/regimes differ and should not be merged: Peters' term (+, subluminal phase velocity) comes from the masses' gravitational field (background curvature, ∝ Gρ, velocity-independent); a shear-rigid medium (Press, §4) gives ω² = c²k² + 16πGμ/c², i.e. n² = 1 − ω_g²/ω² (−, plasma-like, superluminal phase velocity), ω_g² ≡ 16πGμ/c²; kinetic hot-gas results are reported as superluminal ([V] above) while Flauger–Weinberg's CDM effect is described as a slowing. Flat-background kinetic treatments drop background-curvature terms of the same order Gρ. This is my reading; needs checking against the full texts.

## 4. Fundamental limits on absorbing, reflecting, shielding or confining GWs

### Takeaway
Smolin (1984/85) asserts, by case analysis in linearized theory, that no positive-energy material is a good conductor or absorber of GWs over a finite band; Press (1979) shows a GW "metal" needs shear modulus or viscosity so large that it violates a condition all known matter probably satisfies; Garfinkle & Wald (1985) exhibit an in-principle counterexample only at compactness ~1 (charged shell just outside its Schwarzschild radius). Superconductor-mirror claims (Minter, Wegter-McNelly & Chiao) remain an untested, controversial conjecture.

### Cited Findings
- [V] W. H. Press, "On gravitational conductors, waveguides, and circuits", GRG 11, 105–109 (1979), DOI 10.1007/BF00756582 (expanded Gravity Research Foundation essay, honorable mention 1978). Abstract: "a material with sufficiently large elastic shear modulus or shear viscosity will act like a gravitational conductor or 'metal.' It will reflect gravitational waves"; "a gravitational wave can be guided by a single conductor in transverse mode" (no EM analogue); such conductors can obey the dominant energy condition and can be larger than their Schwarzschild radius, but must violate a new condition that is probably satisfied by all existing forms of matter (paraphrase of the abstract's last sentence). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00756582); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1979GReGr..11..105P/abstract)
- [V] L. Smolin, "The thermodynamics of gravitational radiation", GRG 16(3), 205–210 (1984), DOI 10.1007/BF00762535 — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/6722b85744e1e0251e6415cbe0153aaa). As summarized by Padmanabhan & Singh: by considering absorption of classical GWs by classical matter, ionization of atoms by gravitons, and phonon excitation, "no realistic material is an efficient absorber of gravitational radiation, except over a narrow band of frequencies"; Smolin's concern was measurement of the quantum state of the field, which requires absorbed fraction f ≈ 1. — [gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030)
- [V] L. Smolin, "On the intrinsic entropy of the gravitational field", GRG 17, 417–437 (1985), DOI 10.1007/BF00761902. Abstract: "It is shown that in linearized general relativity it is impossible to construct a detector by the use of which the quantum state of the linearized gravitational field could be reliably determined. This is because there is no material satisfying the positive energy condition which can serve as a good conductor or absorber of gravitational radiation over a finite range of frequencies." If true in the full theory, "a certain proportion of both the energy and information carried by a gravitational wave is irreversibly lost", implying an intrinsic entropy of gravitational radiation. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00761902); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/5009688)
- [V] D. Garfinkle & R. Wald, "On the possibility of a box for gravitational radiation", GRG 17(5), 461 (1985): presented as a counter-example to Smolin, "a shell of charged matter balanced just outside its Schwarzschild radius"; they argue a box can be built to confine and thermalize gravitational radiation. — described in [gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030) and in the abstract of [Dell 1987](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00770328)
- [V] J. Dell, "On the impossibility of a box for holding gravitational radiation in thermal equilibrium", GRG 19, 171–177 (1987), DOI 10.1007/BF00770328: argues the Garfinkle–Wald box fails in a universe with external radiation (Springer abstract: external gravitational radiation; Padmanabhan & Singh's paraphrase: the shell is unstable in the presence of electromagnetic radiation — the two descriptions differ). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00770328); [gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030)
- [V] T. Padmanabhan & T. P. Singh, "A note on the thermodynamics of gravitational radiation", CQG 20, 4419 (2003), gr-qc/0305030: linearized gravitational radiation in a cavity can thermalize if the mean radiation density and cavity size satisfy certain constraints; they note that if the box walls are thick enough to absorb all incident radiation the box undergoes gravitational collapse; they treat scattering as matter oscillating under the wave and re-radiating; they present their result as complementary to Smolin's. — [arXiv gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030); [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0264-9381/20/20/307)
- [V] W. D. Smith (unpublished manuscript): Smolin's argument is a collection of designs for would-be gravitational shields with an analysis of each, not a proof of impossibility (paraphrase), though Smith suspects a rigorous version holds. — [rangevoting.org/…/absorber.pdf](https://www.rangevoting.org/WarrenSmithPages/homepage/absorber.pdf)
- [V] S. J. Minter, K. Wegter-McNelly & R. Y. Chiao, "Do mirrors for gravitational waves exist?", Physica E (2010), arXiv:0903.0661: thin superconducting films are predicted to be highly reflective mirrors for GWs at microwave frequencies, via the "Heisenberg-Coulomb effect", which "enormously enhances the interaction of a gravitational wave with a superconductor relative to that of normal matter". — [arXiv:0903.0661](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0661); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386947709001994)
- [V] Status of the claim: an AIP conference paper calls it the "Li-Torr-Chiao conjecture" "because of the controversy surrounding it" — [AIP Conf. Proc. 1208, 467](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/acp/article-abstract/1208/1/467/811278/Testing-the-Li-Torr-Chiao-Conjecture-A-Novel-HFGW); Chiao's own follow-up frames an experiment testing a crucial claim (superluminal supercurrent response) and says that if it is falsified "there would be little point in going ahead" — [arXiv:1011.1326](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.1326). Related: [arXiv:0903.3280](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3280), [arXiv:0904.3956](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.3956); Inan, Thompson et al., "Interaction of gravitational waves with superconductors", Fortschr. Phys. 2017 — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/prop.201600066), [arXiv:2207.08062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08062); review "Superconductors and gravity" — [arXiv:2203.09417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09417).

### Inferences
- [D] **Press's criterion made explicit (my derivation, to be checked against his text).** With TT stress −μh_ij − ηḣ_ij the dispersion relation is ω² = c²k² + (16πG/c²)(μ − iωη). Reflection (evanescence) requires ω_g² ≡ 16πGμ/c² ≳ ω² or 16πGη/c² ≳ ω. For a slab at least a wavelength thick (L ≳ 2πc/ω) that is not inside its Schwarzschild radius (ρ ≲ c²/GL²) this forces μ ≳ (π/4)ρc², i.e. shear-wave speed ≳ c (or kinematic viscosity η/ρ ≳ cL). Ordinary matter has μ/ρc² = (v_s/c)² ~ 10⁻¹⁰. Thus reflectivity of ordinary matter is suppressed by compactness × (v_s/c)², and a true mirror needs stress ~ρc² at compactness ~1 — the regime of Garfinkle & Wald's shell.
- [D] Numbers: steel (μ ≈ 8×10¹⁰ Pa) has ω_g = (16πGμ/c²)^{1/2} ≈ 5×10⁻⁸ s⁻¹ (period ≈ 4 yr): steel is "GW-metallic" only below ~10 nHz, where the wavelength (≳ light-years) exceeds any steel object by ≥15 orders of magnitude.
- The three positions are mutually consistent with the working hypothesis: (Smolin) no good absorber/conductor made of weak-field positive-energy matter; (Press) a conductor needs μ or η beyond a bound real matter obeys; (Garfinkle–Wald, Padmanabhan–Singh) efficient confinement or total absorption is possible only at the threshold of collapse, GM/Rc² → O(1).

### Gaps
- Smolin's quantitative bound (the formula for the maximal absorbed/reflected fraction, in his own symbols) was **not** retrieved — both papers are paywalled at Springer and unreachable here. Whether he states it as "≲ GM/Rc²" is therefore UNVERIFIED.
- Press's exact "new condition" was not seen; the μ ≲ ρc² form above is my reconstruction.
- Garfinkle & Wald's own abstract, their reflection coefficient versus shell radius, and Dell's precise objection were not seen first-hand.
- [U] Rebuttals to the superconductor-mirror claim were not searched (budget). Recollected leads: Kiefer & Weber, "On the interaction of mesoscopic quantum systems with gravity", Ann. Phys. 14, 253 (2005), gr-qc/0408010 (critique of earlier Chiao proposals); Quach, "Gravitational Casimir effect", PRL 114, 081104 (2015), arXiv:1502.07429 (builds on the conjecture; an erratum may exist); Norte, Moura & Gröblacher, PRL 121, 030405 (2018) (Casimir experiment with superconductors constraining Quach's prediction). None verified.
- [U] Graviton-absorption limits not searched: Rothman & Boughn, "Can gravitons be detected?", Found. Phys. 36, 1801 (2006), gr-qc/0601043; Boughn & Rothman, CQG 23, 5839 (2006); Dyson, "Is a graviton detectable?", IJMPA 28, 1330041 (2013), which I recall contains a sum rule of the form ∫σ(ω)dω/ω = 4π²L_P²Q (L_P = Planck length, Q a dimensionless quadrupole measure). If confirmed, that sum rule is the most rigorous general bound on absorption by matter and should be compared with the compactness hypothesis.

## 6. Gravitational lensing and focusing of GWs (wave optics; transparent Sun; Earth/planets)

### Takeaway
Nothing on this topic could be retrieved in this session; standard formulas are given from memory and flagged. Elementary lensing arithmetic (derived here) already shows the compactness scaling: a uniform transparent sphere has central focal length F = R/(6C), focal ratio F/2R = 1/(12C), and focuses at all only for λ ≲ 6GM/c².

### Cited Findings
- [V] Only one adjacent title surfaced: "Wave propagation in a weak gravitational field and the validity of the thin lens approximation" — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/1750393_Wave_propagation_in_a_weak_gravitational_field_and_the_validity_of_the_thin_lens_approximation).

### Inferences
- [D] **Focusing power is column density.** Central rays through a transparent body: α(b) = 4GM(<b)/(c²b) → 4πGΣ₀b/c², so F = c²/(4πGΣ₀), Σ₀ = central column density. Uniform sphere: α = (4GM/c²b)[1 − (1 − b²/R²)^{3/2}] ≈ 6GMb/(c²R²), F_centre = R²c²/(6GM) = R/(6C); limb rays: F_limb = R²c²/(4GM) = R/(4C). C ≡ GM/(Rc²).
- [D] Numbers: Sun limb focus R_⊙²c²/(4GM_⊙) = 8.19×10¹³ m = 548 AU; uniform-density Sun centre focus 365 AU (the real, centrally condensed Sun focuses much closer — see [U] 23.5 AU). Uniform Earth (C = 6.96×10⁻¹⁰): F = 1.5×10¹⁵ m ≈ 1.0×10⁴ AU; with an assumed diametral column density ≈ 1.1×10¹¹ kg/m², F ≈ 9.7×10¹⁴ m ≈ 6500 AU. Any "gravitational camera" on or near Earth is ~10⁸ Earth radii inside the focal distance.
- [D] **Diffraction limit.** Fresnel number at the focus N = R²/(λF) = 6CR/λ, so geometric focusing requires λ ≲ 6GM/c²: Earth 2.7 cm (f ≳ 11 GHz); Sun 8.9 km (f ≳ 34 kHz). Equivalent point-lens parameter w ≡ 8πGMf/c³ = 4πr_s/λ: Earth w = 3.7×10⁻⁸ at 100 Hz; Sun w = 1 at f ≈ 8.1 kHz. For w ≪ 1 the maximum on-axis intensity gain is 1 + πw/2 (from the [U] formula below): Earth at 100 Hz, 1 + 6×10⁻⁸.

### Gaps

Point-mass amplification factor F(w,y) = exp[πw/4 + i(w/2){ln(w/2) − 2φ_m(y)}]·Γ(1 − iw/2)·₁F₁(iw/2, 1; iwy²/2), w = 8πGM_L(1+z_L)f/c³, y = source offset in Einstein radii, φ_m = (x_m − y)²/2 − ln x_m, x_m = [y + (y²+4)^{1/2}]/2; on-axis |F|² = πw/(1 − e^{−πw}); diffraction suppresses lensing when λ ≳ GM_L/c², quoted as M_L ≲ 10⁸ M_⊙ (f/mHz)⁻¹.

### Inferences
- [D] Summary of every attenuation channel for the Earth (C = 7×10⁻¹⁰) at ~1–100 Hz, from the formulas above: viscoelastic absorption ~10⁻¹⁹–10⁻²¹; free-surface conversion to seismic waves ~10⁻²²; refractive phase ~10⁻¹⁰ rad; lensing intensity change ≤ 6×10⁻⁸ (diffraction-limited). ## 8. How much of the compactness-bound hypothesis is already in print

### Takeaway
In print (seen this session): a rigorous viscous-damping law, a heuristic compactness-type ceiling on absorption for self-gravitating systems (Loeb), a weak-field refractive index ∝ Gρ/ω² (Peters), and qualitative no-absorber / no-conductor statements (Smolin, Press) with an explicit counterexample only at compactness ~1 (Garfinkle–Wald). Not found: a single stated inequality bounding opacity, reflectivity, refractive and focusing power of an arbitrary element by O(1)×GM/(Rc²), or any "no shadows / lensless" framing.

### Cited Findings
- **Proven (within linearized GR + hydrodynamics/kinetic theory):** energy damping rate 16πGη (hydrodynamic limit) and its ~1/(ωτ)² suppression when collisionless; maximal collisional damping matters only at horizon-scale wavelengths. — [Baym et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05192); refractive index n − 1 = 2πGρ/ω² — [Peters](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.9.2207).
- **Argued heuristically:** dissipated fraction ≤ 16πGρR²σ/c³ ≈ 36(σ/c)³ for bound systems (order-of-magnitude kinetic estimate η ≲ ρRσ) — [Loeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01730); "no material satisfying the positive energy condition … can serve as a good conductor or absorber of gravitational radiation over a finite range of frequencies" (case-by-case, linearized theory; criticized as not a proof) — [Smolin 1985](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00761902), [Smith critique](https://www.rangevoting.org/WarrenSmithPages/homepage/absorber.pdf); GW conductors must violate a new condition probably satisfied by all existing forms of matter — [Press 1979](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00756582); fully absorbing walls imply collapse — [Padmanabhan & Singh](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030).
- **Known exception at C ~ 1:** charged shell just outside its Schwarzschild radius as a confining box — [Garfinkle & Wald, as described in gr-qc/0305030](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0305030); disputed by [Dell 1987](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00770328).

### Inferences
- [D] Each channel reduces, by short derivations from the printed formulas, to compactness times a factor ≤ 1: absorption ≲ 12C(σ/c) (Loeb) or 16πGμL/(Qωc³) (viscoelastic); reflection ∼ C(v_s/c)² (Press-type dispersion relation); refractive phase ≈ 3C(λ/2πR) (Peters); focusing 1/F = 6C/R, focal ratio 1/(12C), diffraction-limited unless λ ≲ 6GM/c². The unified inequality and the explicit prefactors appear to be new as a statement, though each ingredient is standard.
- The hypothesis should be worded to survive two known caveats: (a) narrow-band resonances (Smolin's "except over a narrow band"; bars gain a factor Q inside ν₀/Q); (b) near-zone / non-plane-wave configurations where damping is enhanced ([arXiv:2605.14956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14956)).

### Gaps
- Whether Smolin (1984/85) or Press (1979) already writes the bound explicitly as a function of GM/(Rc²) is **unknown** — their full texts must be read before claiming novelty.
- Dyson's 2013 sum rule, black-hole cross sections, wave-optics lensing, transparent-Sun focusing and scattering cross sections are all [U] here; sections 5–7 need a second pass with arxiv.org, ui.adsabs.harvard.edu, link.springer.com, inspirehep.net and journals.aps.org allowed in the session's network settings and a fresh search budget.
