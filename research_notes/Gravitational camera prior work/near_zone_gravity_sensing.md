## 1. Newtonian / gravity-gradient noise from everyday objects (Saulson; Hughes & Thorne; Thorne & Winstein; Creighton; Harms)

### Takeaway
The LIGO literature already treats everyday moving masses as near-zone Newtonian tidal sources: a single walking person 10 m from a test mass produces √S_h ≈ 0.6×10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² at 10 Hz (falling as f⁻⁶ and r⁻³), which is why Thorne & Winstein recommended a ~10 m human exclusion zone; a 0.1 kg tumbleweed gives SNR ≈ 4 at 10 Hz in advanced LIGO and SNRs of hundreds if it hits a building. Uniformly moving objects (cars, people, wind-borne air masses) are exponentially suppressed above f ≈ v/(2πd).

### Cited Findings
- **Saulson 1984**, "Terrestrial gravitational noise on a gravitational wave antenna", Phys. Rev. D 30, 732, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.30.732 (LIGO-P840003). Treats fluctuating gravitational forces from ground motion, air-pressure fluctuations and moving massive bodies; estimates the gravitational power spectrum at a point on the Earth's surface; the noise is significant for interferometric antennas below about 10 Hz. — [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1103/physrevd.30.732); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6542819); [LIGO DCC P840003](https://dcc-llo.ligo.org/LIGO-P840003/public)
- Creighton describes Saulson as the first to estimate gravity-gradient noise for terrestrial interferometers (seismic waves and acoustic pressure waves). — [Creighton, ar5iv gr-qc/0007050](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0007050)
- **Hughes & Thorne 1998**, "Seismic gravity-gradient noise in interferometric gravitational-wave detectors", Phys. Rev. D 58, 122002, arXiv:gr-qc/9806018. The noise is expressed through a transfer function from the seismic displacement spectrum to the test-mass motion spectrum, with a dimensionless reduced transfer function β′(f), summed incoherently over seismic modes. Quoted result: "We conclude that at quiet times β′≃0.35–0.6 at the LIGO sites, and at noisy times β′≃0.15–1.4." Saulson's simple model gave β = β′ = 1/√3 = 0.58. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9806018); [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.58.122002); [PRD PDF mirror](https://www.nikhef.nl/~jo/virgo/gw/NN/e122002.pdf)
- Hughes & Thorne: at quiet times the seismic gravity-gradient noise is below the benchmark "advanced LIGO" noise level ("although not by much near 10 Hz"); it may significantly exceed it at noisy times; they suggest shielding the corner and end stations from fundamental-mode Rayleigh waves, which they suspect dominate at quiet times. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9806018)
- **Thorne & Winstein 1999**, "Human gravity-gradient noise in interferometric gravitational-wave detectors", Phys. Rev. D 60, 082001, arXiv:gr-qc/9810016 (submitted Oct 1998). Among routine human activities, walking produces the strongest gravity-gradient noise; it comes from the beginning and end of weight transfer from one foot to the other, which cause sharp (~20 ms) changes in the horizontal jerk of the person's centre of mass. Limb motion contributes little compared with centre-of-mass motion. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9810016); [APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.082001)
- Thorne & Winstein headline formula (band 2.5 Hz ≲ f ≲ 25 Hz, stated accurate to within a factor 3):
  **√S_h(f) ∼ 0.6×10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² (f/10 Hz)⁻⁶ [Σ_i (r_i/10 m)⁻⁶]^{1/2}**, sum over all walking people at distances r_i from the nearest test mass. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9810016); [APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prd/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.082001)
- Thorne & Winstein recommendation: an exclusion zone of r ≃ 10 m around the test masses keeps human gravity-gradient noise negligible for advanced LIGO; the same zone also brings noise from a slamming door and a fist striking a wall to an acceptable level. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9810016)
- Thorne's Nobel lecture restates it: using experimental data on human motion from Winstein's colleagues, "we computed the size of this noise and concluded that, if humans are kept more than 10 meters from a LIGO mirror, the noise is acceptably small." — [Rev. Mod. Phys. 90, 040503](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/pdf/10.1103/RevModPhys.90.040503)
- **Creighton**, "Tumbleweeds and airborne gravitational noise sources for LIGO", arXiv:gr-qc/0007050 (July 2000; published version given in our brief as Class. Quantum Grav. 25, 125011 (2008); the journal reference was not re-confirmed in search). Considers atmospheric density variations, temperature variations advected past the detector, and large airborne objects. The background noise is below the expected noise floor even of advanced detectors, but only by about an order of magnitude for temperature perturbations carried along turbulent streamlines. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0007050)
- Creighton, transients: wind-driven objects such as tumbleweeds could produce signals with SNR in the hundreds if they strike the interferometer buildings, so fences may be needed; "a typical 0.1 kg weed moving at these speeds would give a signal-to-noise ratio of about 4 at 10 Hz in advanced LIGO"; collisions matter far more than objects merely passing by. Reference advanced-LIGO noise floor used: S_h ≈ 2×10⁻⁴⁵ Hz⁻¹ at 10 Hz. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0007050); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0007050)
- Creighton's thesis: neither background source exceeds the advanced-detector noise floor, but sonic booms and debris striking the detector can both produce detectable signals. — [Caltech thesis PDF](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/10400/2/Creighton_t_2000.pdf); later talk on infrasonic NN and atmospheric shock transients: [LIGO-G1500688](https://dcc-lho.ligo.org/public/0119/G1500688/003/atmospheric-nn-talk.pdf)
- **Harms, "Terrestrial Gravity Fluctuations"**, Living Reviews in Relativity: 2015 version (lrr-2015-3, arXiv:1507.05850, PMC5256008) and 2019 update (Living Rev. Relativ. vol. 22, DOI 10.1007/s41114-019-0022-2). It "derives and analyzes models of terrestrial gravity perturbations related to seismic fields, atmospheric disturbances, and vibrating, rotating or moving objects". Section 6 is "Gravity perturbations from objects", with 6.1 "Rules of thumb for gravity perturbations" (to estimate relevance "by eye" before calculating; explicitly not a guarantee) and 6.2 "Objects moving with constant speed". — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256008/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05850); [Springer 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41114-019-0022-2); [lrr-2015-3 mirror](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/LRG/Articles/lrr-2015-3/)
- **Harms et al., "Low-frequency terrestrial gravitational-wave detectors"** (arXiv:1308.2074): models Newtonian noise from an object moving at constant speed v on a straight line past a test mass at closest-approach distance r; the spectrum involves a modified Bessel function of the second kind (their Eq. 5); such noise "would completely dominate the signal if it was not for the exponential suppression, which is especially effective for underground detectors"; rule: noise from uniformly moving objects can be neglected above f ≈ v/(2πr). The object "could be a car, a person, or an atmospheric density fluctuation carried by wind". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2074)
- Recent related titles surfaced (content not read): "Atmospheric Newtonian Noise May Constrain Third-Generation Gravitational-Wave Detectors" (arXiv:2505.05690, 2025) and "Prospects for Observing Gravity-gradient Noise and Earthquake Gravity Signals with CHRONOS" (arXiv:2606.19124, 2026). — [arXiv:2505.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05690); [arXiv:2606.19124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19124)

### Inferences
- **[D] Walking person, converted to test-mass acceleration.** With h = δx/L and L = 4 km, the Thorne–Winstein value at 10 Hz, 10 m is δx ≈ 2.4×10⁻²⁰ m/√Hz, i.e. δa = (2πf)² δx ≈ 9.5×10⁻¹⁷ m s⁻²/√Hz. Scaling r⁻³ to 1 m gives ≈ 1×10⁻¹³ m s⁻²/√Hz, i.e. ≈ 1×10⁻¹⁶ N/√Hz on a 1 g mass; a search snippet quoted exactly this "1.0×10⁻¹⁶ N/√Hz at 10 Hz for a person moving 1 m away" from an unidentified citing paper, so the conversion is consistent.
- **[D] Why the spectrum is so steep.** Walking has a ~2 Hz fundamental; only the ~20 ms jerk discontinuities reach 10 Hz, hence f⁻⁶. A camera pixel with LIGO-grade sensitivity at 1–3 Hz would see a walker enormously more strongly: the formula's f⁻⁶ gives a factor 10⁶ between 10 Hz and 1 Hz (outside its stated 2.5–25 Hz validity band, so indicative only).
- **[D] Uniformly moving point mass (standard result, same integral as the Coulomb field of a passing charge).** For mass m, speed v, closest approach d, the Fourier transforms of the acceleration components at the detector are |ã_⊥(ω)| = (2Gmω/v²) K₁(ωd/v) and |ã_∥(ω)| = (2Gmω/v²) K₀(ωd/v); for ωd/v ≫ 1 both fall as (2Gmω/v²)√(πv/2ωd) e^{−ωd/v}. This reproduces the "exponential suppression" and the f ≈ v/(2πd) corner quoted by Harms et al. Example: car at v = 20 m/s, d = 10 m has corner 0.32 Hz; at 10 Hz the suppression factor is e^{−31} ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁴. Constant-velocity motion is essentially invisible in the audio band; only accelerations, impacts, vibration and rotation matter.
- The three source classes in the LIGO literature map onto camera "scene content": (i) accelerating compact masses (people, doors, tumbleweed impacts), (ii) density waves in continuous media (seismic Rayleigh waves with β′ ~ 0.4–0.6; infrasound; advected temperature inhomogeneities), (iii) uniformly moving masses (suppressed).

## 2. Deliberately generated dynamic gravity fields detected by GW detectors and laboratory experiments

### Takeaway
Rotating or vibrating laboratory masses of ~0.1–50 kg at ~1–10 m have been detected through their near-zone tidal field since 1967 (Sinsky & Weber at 1660 Hz; Hirakawa et al. out to 4.2 m and later 10.7 m; the Explorer bar at SNR ≈ 20), and today kg-scale rotors 1.3–1.9 m from a test mass are used as sub-percent absolute calibrators (NCal/GCal) of Virgo, LIGO and KAGRA. At the other extreme, the field of a 90 mg gold sphere has been measured at 12.7 mHz and beam–beam coupling at 42 Hz.

### Cited Findings
- **Sinsky & Weber 1967**, Phys. Rev. Lett. 18, 795–797 (8 May 1967). Published title is "New Source for *Dynamical* Gravitational Fields". Source: an acoustically stressed volume of matter (an aluminium cylinder stressed nearly to breaking); fields measured as a function of distance and azimuthal angle with apparatus responsive to the curvature tensor; operating frequency 1660 Hz; described in reviews as a "high-frequency dynamical Cavendish experiment". — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/4666222ce9dc99c0f54179cc161e9002); [Physics Today review](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/gravitational-waves-a-progress-report)
- **Hirakawa, Tsubono & Oide 1980**, "Dynamical test of the law of gravitation", Nature 283, 184–185, DOI 10.1038/283184a0. Tested gravitation over separations up to 4.2 m using "a low frequency dynamical quadrupole–quadrupole interaction between two masses, an effect first observed by Sinsky and Weber". Newton's law verified within about 3% (secondary report). A follow-up by the same group used a low-frequency dynamic field over R = 2.6–10.7 m. — [Nature PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/283184a0.pdf); [Christian Science Monitor 1981](https://www.csmonitor.com/1981/0902/090235.html); [follow-up, OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/scitech/biblio/6707783); related group papers [PRD 32, 342](https://www.doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.32.342), [PRD 36, 2321](https://www.doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.36.2321)
- **Astone et al. 1991**, "Evaluation and preliminary measurement of the interaction of a dynamical gravitational near field with a cryogenic gravitational wave antenna", Z. Phys. C 50, 21–29, DOI 10.1007/BF01558552. Field of a small rotating quadrupole acting on the 2270 kg Explorer cryogenic antenna (Rome group); induced signal 20 times larger than detector noise with the FET amplifier; "with this method we were able to make an absolute calibration of a gravitational wave antenna". — [DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/BF01558552)
- **Astone et al. 1998**, "Experimental study of the dynamic Newtonian field with a cryogenic gravitational wave antenna", Eur. Phys. J. C 5, 651–664, DOI 10.1007/s100529800987. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s100529800987)
- **Forward's rotating gravity gradiometer (Hughes Research Laboratories).** A 1971 Hughes final report states that a laboratory research model demonstrated the engineering feasibility of the rotating gravity gradiometer concept; a 1982 report lists the RGG at about 1.0 E demonstrated (10 s integration), with ~0.1–0.3 E projected (table garbled in the extraction). Patent: US 3,564,921 "Torsionally resonant gravity gradient sensor". — [NASA NTRS 19720010678](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720010678/downloads/19720010678.pdf); [NASA NTRS 19830002134](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19830002134/downloads/19830002134.pdf); [US3564921A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3564921A/Direct)
- **Virgo NCal, first tests: Estevez et al. 2018**, "First tests of a Newtonian calibrator on an interferometric gravitational wave detector", Class. Quantum Grav. 35, 235009, arXiv:1806.06572. "We report tests of a calibration technique, based on the well-known gravitational force, which has been applied for the first time on a large interferometer"; it uses "the variation of the Newtonian gravitational field produced by moving masses to induce a known displacement of the interferometer mirrors". Simplest model: two masses m separated by 2r on a rotor at frequency f, at distance d from a mirror of mass M. — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018CQGra..35w5009E); [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1806.06572)
- **Virgo O3 NCal** ("Newtonian calibrator tests during the Virgo O3 data taking", arXiv:2011.03728): rotor external diameter 205 mm; the signal is ∝ 1/d⁴, so the mirror–NCal distance is critical; expected distances 1.267 m (near NCal) and 1.947 m (far NCal); a 6.4 mm distance uncertainty gives 2.02% (near) and 1.31% (far) on h_rec/h_inj; rotor geometry contributes 0.53%. — [arXiv:2011.03728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03728)
- **Virgo O4 NCal** ("The Virgo Newtonian calibration system for the O4 observing run", arXiv:2406.10028): six rotors on three axes around the north-end mirror; relative standard uncertainty on the NCal-induced displacement amplitude 0.17%; a later O4 calibration paper reports 0.12% after replacing an aluminium rotor pair with composite rotors, and that the NCal was used as *the reference* for Virgo calibration with the photon calibrator adjusted to match. O4 strain uncertainty: below 2.5% in modulus over 10 Hz–1 kHz and within 25 mrad in phase below 200 Hz. — [arXiv:2406.10028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10028); [arXiv:2511.12566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12566); [arXiv:2607.19273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19273)
- **LIGO NCal: Ross et al. 2021**, "Initial results from the LIGO Newtonian calibrator", Phys. Rev. D 104, 082006, arXiv:2107.00141 (LIGO-P1900244). A rotor with quadrupole and hexapole mass distributions exerts a time-varying gravitational force on a test mass, predictable to better than 1% relative uncertainty; installed at the X-end of LIGO Hanford during the O3 commissioning break; force appears at 2f and 3f of the rotor frequency; each multipole has a different distance dependence; rotor torques also couple to the readout through beam offset from the test-mass centre. Table values: tungsten cylinder mass ≈ 1.0558 kg, test mass ≈ 39.680 kg. Used as a cross-check of, not a replacement for, the photon calibrator. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00141); [LIGO DCC](https://dcc.ligo.org/LIGO-P1900244/public)
- Follow-up: "A Tale of Two Calibrators: Comparing Newtonian and Photon Calibration in Advanced LIGO after the Third Observing Run" (arXiv:2609.01917, 2026): the single-rotor system's precision is limited by geometric uncertainty, especially the rotor-to-test-mass position. — [arXiv:2609.01917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01917)
- **KAGRA GCal: Inoue et al. 2018**, "Improving the absolute accuracy of the gravitational wave detectors by combining the photon pressure and gravity field calibrators", arXiv:1804.08249. Combines a photon calibrator with a gravity-field calibrator; uses quadrupole plus hexapole mass arrangements to remove the distance uncertainty; estimated absolute uncertainty 0.17%, about ten times better than previous methods. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08249); [KAGRA GCal wiki](https://gwwiki.icrr.u-tokyo.ac.jp/JGWwiki/KAGRA/Subgroups/CAL/Gcal)
- Precursor concept: Matone et al., "Benefits of artificially generated gravity gradients for interferometric gravitational-wave detectors" (arXiv:gr-qc/0701134). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0701134)
- **Westphal, Hepach, Pfaff & Aspelmeyer 2021**, "Measurement of gravitational coupling between millimetre-sized masses", Nature (2021) pp. 225–228, DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-03250-7. Two gold spheres of ~1 mm radius and 90 mg; source mass position modulated at 12.7 mHz; miniature torsion pendulum read out off resonance; linear and quadratic coupling observed, consistent with a time-varying 1/r potential; systematic accuracy 4×10⁻¹¹ m/s², statistical precision 4×10⁻¹² m/s² in 350 h; non-gravitational forces below ~10% of the signal; minimum surface separation 400 µm. — [Aspelmeyer group summary](https://aspelmeyer.univie.ac.at/details/news/measuring-the-gravitational-field-of-a-milligram-mass); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03250-7)

Two beams in their first bending resonance near 42 Hz; acoustic/mechanical/electrical isolation, heterodyne laser interferometry and lock-in detection give sub-picometre resolution; inverse-square dependence of detector amplitude on beam distance observed; G extracted "a bit higher than the CODATA value" with a suspected systematic (slight mechanical coupling); near-field gravitational energy flow evaluated; described as the first dynamical gravity experiment in this frequency range with a full quantitative theory–experiment comparison. Follow-up with two rotating bars as transmitters: arXiv:2301.01644; a 2026 APS talk covers "Dynamic Gravity Revisited in the Hz Regime, incl. Shielding". — [arXiv:2201.03420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03420); [ETH record](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/handle/20.500.11850/557791); [arXiv:2301.01644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01644); [APS 2026 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/smt/2026/vir-h02/2/)

### Inferences
- **[D] Two-mass rotor formula (re-derived; I believe it matches Estevez et al.'s leading-order expression but could not re-open the paper).** Two point masses m at ±r on a rotor whose plane contains the line to a point test mass at distance d ≫ r: quadrupole potential Φ = −G m r² (1 + 3cos2θ)/(2d³), so the longitudinal acceleration is a = (3Gmr²/2d⁴)(1 + 3cos2θ), with oscillating amplitude **a₂f = 9 G m r² / (2 d⁴)** at twice the rotation frequency. Test-mass displacement amplitude x = a₂f/(2π f_sig)² and strain h = x/L, i.e. **h(2f_rot) = 9 G m r² / (32π² f_rot² L d⁴)**. The d⁻⁴ law agrees with the "signal ∝ 1/d⁴" statement of the O3 paper.
- **[D] Illustrative size** (assumed round numbers, not the actual rotor): m = 1 kg, r = 0.1 m, d = 1.3 m gives a₂f ≈ 1.05×10⁻¹² m/s²; at f_sig = 20 Hz, x ≈ 6.7×10⁻¹⁷ m and h ≈ 2.2×10⁻²⁰ for L = 3 km. So a kg-scale mass moving ~10 cm at ~1 m range is a ≥10³σ-per-second signal for a 10⁻²³/√Hz instrument, which is why it works as a calibrator.
- The hexapole-plus-quadrupole trick (Inoue; Ross) is an existing example of *multipole-resolved near-field sensing*: different multipoles have different d-dependence (d⁻⁴ vs d⁻⁵ for the force), so their ratio measures distance. This is the one-pixel version of ranging in a gravitational camera.

## 3. Prompt elasto-gravity signals (PEGS)

### Takeaway
Gravity perturbations from earthquake rupture travel at light speed and have been observed before P-wave arrival: marginally for Tohoku-Oki 2011 in a superconducting gravimeter (Montagner et al. 2016), clearly on broadband seismometers 1,000–2,000 km away at the ~1 nm/s² level (Vallée et al. 2017), and they are now used in deep-learning early-warning schemes (PEGSNet 2022; operational prototype in Peru). This is the best-established example of a natural, time-varying near-zone Newtonian signal being used as an information channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Harms et al. 2015**, "Transient gravity perturbations induced by earthquake rupture", Geophys. J. Int. 201(3), 1416–1425, DOI 10.1093/gji/ggv090. Analytical solution for gravity perturbations produced by a point double-couple source in a homogeneous, infinite, non-self-gravitating elastic medium (compact closed-form expression); used to assess high-sensitivity gravity strainmeters. — [author PDF](https://www.ipgp.fr/~jpm/PUBLICATIONS/15Harms-GJI-grav_Theory.pdf); [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/hs6t9-kcm59)
- **Montagner et al. 2016**, "Prompt gravity signal induced by the 2011 Tohoku-Oki earthquake", Nat. Commun. 7, 13349, DOI 10.1038/ncomms13349. Searched Kamioka superconducting-gravimeter data and five nearby broadband seismometers for a signal before the P arrival; the paper's own text says a preliminary analysis suggested a transient gravity signal "whose amplitude does not obviously stand above the background microseism noise". A conference abstract quantifies the detection as ≈ −0.2 to −0.3 µGal at 500 km, "just above the detection threshold". Secondary report: shift detectable ~65 s after rupture onset, ~9 s before first P waves. — [author PDF](https://www.ipgp.fr/~jpm/PUBLICATIONS/16Montagner-Natcomm.pdf); [EGU2017-8431](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2017/EGU2017-8431.pdf); [Earth Magazine](https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/gravity-changes-may-warn-large-earthquakes/)
- Criticism: Heaton (2017) objected that "their data analysis did not include the appropriate response of the Earth". A 2019 Earth, Planets and Space paper re-analysed Japanese multi-instrument data (see Gaps for a discrepancy in how its result was summarised). — [EPS 2019, DOI 10.1186/s40623-019-1006-x](https://earth-planets-space.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s40623-019-1006-x); [Earth Magazine](https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/gravity-changes-may-warn-large-earthquakes/)
- **Vallée et al. 2017**, "Observations and modeling of the elastogravity signals preceding direct seismic waves", Science 358, 1164–1168, DOI 10.1126/science.aao0746. Prompt signals from the 2011 Mw 9.1 Tohoku earthquake recorded on broadband seismometers before direct seismic waves; modelled accurately "by a new formalism, taking into account both the gravity changes and the gravity-induced motion"; "the seismometer therefore records a prompt elastogravity signal, due to the direct and induced effects of the gravity perturbations". Waveform selection required the pre-event noise to stay within ±0.8 nm/s² for 30 min. Example station MDJ (NE China), 1280 km from the source, where direct waves arrive ~165 s after origin. ## 4. Gravity gradiometry as an imaging modality

### Takeaway
Existing gravity "imaging" is static, coarse and gradient-limited: field gradiometers work at ~1–20 E per measurement (airborne FTG ~3–15 E/√Hz; the Birmingham atom gradiometer 20 E per point) and resolve metre-scale voids at metre-scale depth. The phrase "gravity camera" appears only in a simulation-level patent; I found no published device or theory treating dynamic gravitational imaging of everyday scenes, and no head-to-head comparison with muon tomography.

(Caveat: a real LIGO compares test masses 4 km apart, so for a source at r ≪ 4 km only the near test mass responds and the relevant quantity is δa/(ω²L), not a local gradient.)
- The existing modality is quasi-static (DC maps, drift-limited); the camera concept differs by using the AC band (Hz–kHz), where instrument noise in strain is far lower but only *time-varying* mass multipoles are visible.
- Gravity couples to density only; muons to density × path and Z. A fair comparison (not found in the literature searched) would be: gravity has no shielding and no source needed but resolution degrades exponentially with standoff (Section 5); muons give geometric-optics resolution but need long exposures.

## 5. Inverse-problem theory for potential fields: non-uniqueness, downward continuation, resolution ∝ depth / ln(SNR)

### Takeaway
Reconstructing sources from a harmonic field is exponentially ill-posed: a spatial frequency k is attenuated by e^{−kz} at standoff z, so the stability of continuation toward the sources is at best logarithmic in the data error. The cleanest *explicit* published "resolution = standoff / log(SNR)" statement found is from near-field acoustic holography: Δx_min ≈ 27.3·d/SNR_dB = π d / ln(SNR_amplitude) (Maynard, Williams & Lee 1985 and later NAH papers); the rigorous PDE counterpart is the logarithmic stability of the Cauchy problem for elliptic equations (Alessandrini, Rondi, Rosset & Vessella 2009).

### Cited Findings
- Downward continuation of potential-field data "is unstable and will enhance noise as well as detail"; the power at high wavenumbers is boosted relative to low wavenumbers; the frequency-domain operator is taken from Blakely (1995, p. 320). — [Cooper, "The stable downward continuation of potential field data", Exploration Geophysics 2004](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-stable-downward-continuation-of-potential-field-data-58gpovp0bo.pdf)
- "If measurement errors are primarily short-wavelength, then the nature of downward continuation operator which amplifies primarily the short-wavelength noise becomes a problem" (citing Blakely 1995 among others); stabilisation by Wiener filtering and multiscale-edge methods. — [Trompat, Boschetti & Hornby, Exploration Geophysics 34](https://www.per.marine.csiro.au/staff/Fabio.Boschetti/papers/herve_eg34_4_boschetti.pdf)
- Blakely, *Potential Theory in Gravity and Magnetic Applications*, Cambridge University Press, 1995 (bibliographic details as cited in the Springer Encyclopedia entry). — [Springer encyclopedia entry](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-1-4020-4423-6_311)
- **Logarithmic stability (rigorous).** Alessandrini, Rondi, Rosset & Vessella, "The stability for the Cauchy problem for elliptic equations", Inverse Problems 25 (2009) 123004, arXiv:0907.2882: if the solution is to be recovered in its whole domain of definition, the best possible rate of stability is logarithmic; interior estimates are of Hölder type; all such results follow from a single building block, the three-spheres inequality. — [arXiv:0907.2882](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2882)
- Related: Bourgeois (2010), conditional stability for ill-posed Cauchy problems for Laplace's equation in C^{1,1} domains. — [ESAIM: M2AN 44, 715](https://www.numdam.org/item/M2AN_2010__44_4_715_0)
- **Explicit resolution formula (near-field acoustic holography).** Attributed to Maynard, Williams & Lee (1985): cut-off resolution Δx_c = 1 / √[(2/λ)² + (SNR·ln10 / (20π D))²], with SNR the measurement signal-to-noise ratio in dB and D the reconstruction (standoff) distance; in the low-frequency limit the resolution is independent of wavelength: **Δx_min ≈ 27.3 D / SNR**. — [J. Sound Vib. 2017, "An experimental comparison of various methods of nearfield acoustic holography"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022460X17303930); ["Enhanced nearfield acoustic holography for larger distances of reconstructions" (OSTI)](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1339568)

- **Magnetostatic analog.** Roth, Sepulveda & Wikswo, "Using a magnetometer to image a two-dimensional current distribution", J. Appl. The general biomagnetic inverse problem has no unique solution, but the 2-D current case does. — [Vanderbilt-hosted PDF](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/lsp/documents/jap-roth-using-89.pdf); [Scilit abstract](https://scilit.com/publications/76f7c8ac2cec40f6717c6a4eb6e67793)

### Inferences
- **[D] The NAH formula is exactly "depth / ln(SNR)".** 27.3 = 20π/ln 10. Writing the amplitude signal-to-noise ratio as A = 10^{SNR_dB/20}, the highest wavenumber still above noise after decaying by e^{−k d} is k_max = ln(A)/d, so the half-period resolution is **Δx_min = π/k_max = π d / ln(A)**, equivalently λ_min = 2π d / ln(A). Halving the standoff halves Δx; halving Δx at fixed standoff requires *squaring* the SNR. This is the citable statement to carry over to gravity.
- **[D] Transfer to gravity.** Above all sources the Newtonian potential obeys Laplace's equation, so each horizontal Fourier component behaves as Φ̃(k, z) = Φ̃(k, 0) e^{−|k| z}; the same holds for g and for the gradient tensor (which merely adds factors of k and k², i.e. algebraic prefactors that do not change the exponential law). Hence for a sensor plane at distance z from a source layer, resolution Δx ≈ π z / ln(SNR_eff), where SNR_eff is the amplitude SNR of the source's spectral content at the cut-off wavenumber. Even SNR = 10²⁰ gives only ln(SNR) ≈ 46, i.e. Δx ≈ z/15. A gravitational camera therefore has resolution of order (a few to ~10)% of the standoff distance, almost independent of pixel sensitivity; there is no analogue of a lens because there is no propagating wave in the near zone.
- **[D] Two distinct ill-posednesses must be kept separate:** (i) *instability* (exponential, above), which regularisation and SNR address logarithmically; (ii) *non-uniqueness*: the exterior field of a static mass distribution determines only its harmonic moments, so infinitely many interior densities produce the same exterior field (e.g. any spherically symmetric redistribution, or an equivalent surface layer). Time dependence, positivity, known density, or compact-support priors are what make "imaging" possible. Roth et al. make the same point for magnetometry: unique only after restricting to 2-D sources.

## 6. Linearized field of an oscillating quadrupole across near, intermediate and wave zones; tidal tensors E_ij and B_ij

### Takeaway
The gauge-invariant observables are the tidal (gravitoelectric) tensor E_ij = R_0i0j and the frame-drag (gravitomagnetic) tensor B_ij, which obey Maxwell-like equations (Maartens & Bassett 1998; Nichols et al. 2011). For a mass quadrupole of angular frequency ω, E_ij is r⁻⁵ times a fourth-degree polynomial in r/ƛ with ƛ = c/ω = λ/2π: the r⁻⁵ term is the retarded Newtonian tide, the r⁻¹ term is the gravitational wave, and the transition is at r ≈ ƛ (4,770 km at 10 Hz), so everyday scenes sit at r/ƛ ~ 10⁻⁶ where the radiative part is ~10⁻²³ of the Newtonian part.

### Cited Findings
- **Thorne 1980**, "Multipole expansions of gravitational radiation", Rev. Mod. Phys. 52, 299–339, DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.52.299. Abstract: "This paper brings together, into a single unified notation, the multipole formalisms for gravitational radiation which various people have constructed... The generic radiation field in the local wave zone is decomposed into multipole components. The energy, linear momentum, and angular momentum in the waves are expressed as infinite sums of multipole contributions." Source multipole moments are given as volume integrals (power series in a small parameter for slow-motion sources), and the near-zone field is matched to an outgoing-wave radiation field. "The concept of 'local wave zone' is introduced to facilitate a clean separation of 'wave generation' from 'wave propagation.'" — [APS](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/RevModPhys.52.299); [PDF mirror](https://blackholes.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/gritting/pdf/gravitational_waves/Thorne_Multipole-expansions-of-gravitational-radiation.pdf)
- **Nichols et al. 2011**, "Visualizing spacetime curvature via frame-drag vortexes and tidal tendexes: General theory and weak-gravity applications", Phys. Rev. D 84, 124014, arXiv:1108.5486. The electric part of the Weyl tensor (tidal field E_ij) governs tidal tendexes and the magnetic part (frame-drag field B_ij) governs frame-drag vortexes. Section VI treats rotating and oscillating current quadrupoles and the corresponding mass quadrupoles in linearized theory: "An oscillating current quadrupole ejects sequences of vortex loops that acquire accompanying tendex loops as they travel, and become current-quadrupole gravitational waves; and similarly for an oscillating mass quadrupole." The mass quadrupole's tidal field is obtained from its frame-drag field (their Eq. 69) using the third of the Maxwell-like equations (their Eq. 15); at large r the 1/r² part of the field is treated as a perturbation of the transverse (radiative) lines. — [arXiv:1108.5486](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.5486); [PRD](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevD.84.124014); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011PhRvD..84l4014N)
- **Maartens & Bassett 1998**, "Gravito-electromagnetism", Class. Quantum Grav. 15, 705–717, arXiv:gr-qc/9704059. "We develop and apply a fully covariant 1 + 3 electromagnetic analogy for gravity. The free gravitational field is covariantly characterized by the Weyl gravito-electric and gravito-magnetic spatial tensor fields, whose dynamical equations are the Bianchi identities. Using a covariant generalization of spatial vector algebra and calculus to spatial tensor fields, we exhibit the covariant analogy between the tensor Bianchi equations and the vector Maxwell equations." The equations are invariant under spatial duality rotations (as source-free Maxwell); a super-energy density and super-Poynting vector (Bel–Robinson) are constructed. — [arXiv:gr-qc/9704059](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9704059); [Portsmouth record](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/gravito-electromagnetism/)

### Inferences
- **[D] Explicit linearized tidal field of a time-dependent mass quadrupole (derived here; cross-check against Nichols et al. Sec. VI).** Conventions: G and c restored at the end; I_ab(t) = ∫ρ (x_a x_b − ⅓δ_ab x²) d³x (trace-free); F_ab ≡ I_ab(t − r/c)/r; radiation field h^TT_ij = (2G/c⁴ r) Ï^TT_ij. Requiring E_ij to be symmetric, trace-free and divergence-free in vacuum (the Maxwell-like constraint ∂_i E_ij = 0), to reduce to ∂_i∂_jΦ_Newton in the static limit and to −½ ḧ^TT_ij in the wave zone fixes it uniquely:
  **E_ij = G [ −½ ∂_i∂_j∂_a∂_b F_ab + (2/c²) ∂_t² ∂_a∂_(i F_j)a − (1/c⁴) ∂_t⁴ F_ij − (1/2c²) δ_ij ∂_t² ∂_a∂_b F_ab ]**.
  I checked that (a) the trace and divergence vanish identically using □F_ab = 0, (b) the wave-zone limit is −(G/c⁴ r)[I⁽⁴⁾_ij]^TT, (c) this agrees term by term with the form I recall from Nichols et al. for the current quadrupole after the duality substitution (UNVERIFIED recollection: B_ij = ⅔[−(S_pq/r)_,pqij + ε_ipq(⁽²⁾S_pm/r)_,qn ε_jmn + 2(⁽²⁾S_p(i/r)_,j)p − ⁽⁴⁾S_ij/r], with S_pq → ¾ I_pq, B → E).
- **[D] The scalar building block shows the three "Newtonian-potential" zones:** ∂_a∂_b[I_ab(t−r/c)/r] = n_a n_b [3 I_ab/r³ + 3 İ_ab/(c r²) + Ï_ab/(c² r)], so the retarded quadrupole potential is Φ = −(G/2) n_a n_b [3I_ab/r³ + 3İ_ab/(c r²) + Ï_ab/(c² r)]_ret. For harmonic time dependence e^{−iωt} the bracket is (I_ab/r³)[3 − 3i(kr) − (kr)²] e^{ikr}, k = ω/c, i.e. the ℓ = 2 outgoing spherical Hankel function.
- **[D] Longitudinal tidal component, all orders.** Contracting the formula with n_i n_j: **E_rr = −G [18 I_nn/r⁵ + 18 İ_nn/(c r⁴) + 6 Ï_nn/(c² r³)]_ret**, with I_nn = n_a n_b I_ab; the r⁻² and r⁻¹ terms cancel identically (the radiation is transverse). Harmonic form: E_rr = −(18 G I_nn/r⁵)[1 − i kr − (kr)²/3] e^{i(kr−ωt)}. Check: two masses m at ±a on the axis give I_nn = (4/3) m a² and E_rr = −24 G m a²/r⁵, equal to the direct Newtonian expansion.
- **[D] General structure.** For an ℓ-pole of frequency ω every component of E_ij (and B_ij) is e^{i(kr−ωt)} r^{−(ℓ+3)} × [polynomial of degree ℓ+2 in kr] × angular tensor. For ℓ = 2: powers r⁻⁵ (quasi-static Newtonian tide), r⁻⁴, r⁻³ (induction/intermediate), r⁻², r⁻¹ (radiative, transverse-traceless). B_ij is one order of (kr) down in the near zone: B ~ (G İ/c r⁴), i.e. |B|/|E| ~ kr = r/ƛ there, and |B| = |E| in the wave zone.
- **[D] Transition radius ƛ = λ/2π = c/ω:** 47,700 km at 1 Hz; 4,770 km at 10 Hz; 477 km at 100 Hz; 47.7 km at 1 kHz (λ itself: 3×10⁵ km to 300 km). Ratio of the would-be radiative term to the Newtonian term at distance r is ~(kr)⁴/18: for r = 10 m at 10 Hz, kr = 2.1×10⁻⁶ and (kr)⁴ ≈ 1.9×10⁻²³. Everything a camera pixel registers from an everyday scene is the first (r⁻⁵, more generally r^{−(ℓ+3)}) term; gravitomagnetic B_ij is smaller by ~10⁻⁶ and radiation by ~10⁻²³.
- **[D] Pixel readout.** Geodesic deviation ξ̈_i = −E_ij ξ_j gives, for an L-shaped pixel much smaller than r with arms along x and y, ḧ_readout = −(E_xx − E_yy); for a gravitational wave E_ij = −½ḧ^TT_ij and this reduces to h_readout = h_+. A full-tensor pixel would measure all five independent components of E_ij.

## 7. Retardation and the "speed of gravity" in the near zone

### Takeaway
In general relativity the near-zone field of a moving source shows almost no aberration because velocity-dependent terms cancel the retardation (Carlip 2000); the only claimed "speed of gravity" measurement before GW170817-type tests, Fomalont & Kopeikin's Jupiter deflection (c_g = 1.06 ± 0.21 c), was disputed by Will, Samuel and Asada as really measuring the speed of light. Laboratory proposals to see near-zone retardation exist (Paranjape's two-mass null arrangement; a rotating-mass phase method) but none has been carried out.

### Cited Findings
- **Carlip 2000**, "Aberration and the speed of gravity", Phys. Lett. A 267, 81–87, arXiv:gr-qc/9909087. Responds to Van Flandern's claim that the observed absence of gravitational aberration requires "Newtonian" gravity to propagate at c_g > 2×10¹⁰ c. "Aberration in general relativity is almost exactly canceled by velocity-dependent interactions, permitting c_g = c. This cancellation is dictated by conservation laws and the quadrupole nature of gravitational radiation." The field of an accelerating mass is evaluated using Kinnersley's "photon rocket". — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9909087); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/9909087); [Usenet Physics FAQ](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/physics/Relativity/GR/grav_speed.html)
- **Fomalont & Kopeikin 2003 (radio-wave deflection/delay by Jupiter).** Reported the speed of gravity as 1.06 ± 0.21 times the speed of light; the measured effect was a delay of radio waves by Jupiter's gravity of order 5 picoseconds. — [APS News, June 2003](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2003/06/physicist-disputes-speed-of-gravity-claim); [Physics Today, 2003](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/can-the-speed-of-gravity)
- **Controversy.** Will: "it's not possible to determine the speed of gravity from these light-delay observations" (the velocity-dependent term depends on the speed of light, the c_g-dependence being higher order and too small to measure). Samuel: the experiment measured effects associated with the propagation of light, and the effects of Jupiter's motion on the delay are at least 100, perhaps 1,000, times smaller than the array could detect. Asada reached the same "speed of light" interpretation. Kopeikin (and Fomalont) maintained that the experiment tests the fundamental speed of gravity. — [APS News](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2003/06/physicist-disputes-speed-of-gravity-claim); [Berkeley Lab release](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2003/06/18/berkeley-lab-physicist-challenges-speed-of-gravity-claim/); [Kopeikin, gr-qc/0310065](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0310065); [Kopeikin & Fomalont, astro-ph/0311063](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0311063); [Kopeikin & Fomalont, "Gravitomagnetism and the speed of gravity", gr-qc/0507001](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0507001)
- **Laboratory proposals.** Paranjape and co-authors ("Near Zone Dynamical Effects in Gravity", arXiv:1208.2293; "A proposal to measure the speed of gravity"): "two masses placed at two different distances from a detector of gravitational force... placed so that the static gravitational force they produce at the detector exactly cancels. We show that if the masses are made to oscillate, then the force on the detector no longer vanishes and is proportional to the relative time delay", inversely proportional to the speed of gravity. — [arXiv:1208.2293](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2293); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/22772432)
- A 2022 paper in Advances in High Energy Physics (arXiv:2204.14210) analyses a short-distance experiment in which the phase of the detector vibration relative to a rotating-mass emitter is used to calculate the speed of gravity. — [arXiv:2204.14210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14210)
- Related laboratory light-propagation tests: feasibility of measuring a Shapiro delay over metre scales with a rotating mass (Syracuse); a later study finds Cosmic Explorer/Einstein Telescope could detect a rotating-mass Shapiro-delay signal because rotation gives a phase-coherent periodic signal; a 2026 proposal, "Terrestrial Test of Shapiro Time Delay" (arXiv:2601.00468). No completed laboratory measurement of gravitational retardation was found. — [Syracuse](https://surface.syr.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1011&context=phy); [NSF PAR 10197256](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10197256); [arXiv:2601.00468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00468)

### Inferences
- **[D] Size of near-zone retardation for a conserved quadrupole source.** From Section 6, the longitudinal tide is ∝ [1 − ikr − (kr)²/3] e^{ikr} = 1 + (kr)²/6 + (kr)⁴/24 + i (kr)⁵/45 + …: the terms linear and cubic in kr cancel exactly, so the field tracks the *instantaneous* source configuration with an amplitude correction (kr)²/6 and a phase lag of only (kr)⁵/45 (general ℓ: (kr)^{2ℓ+1}/[(2ℓ+1)!!(2ℓ−1)!!], the ratio j_ℓ/y_ℓ). This is the multipole-language version of Carlip's cancellation. For r = 10 m, f = 10 Hz: kr = 2.1×10⁻⁶, amplitude correction 7×10⁻¹³, phase lag ≈ 9×10⁻³¹ rad; at 1 kHz and 10 m, kr = 2.1×10⁻⁴, phase lag ≈ 9×10⁻²¹ rad. Naive "light-travel-time" phase kr (2×10⁻⁶ rad) does not appear in the observable.
- Consequently a gravitational camera at everyday scales cannot time-gate or range by propagation delay; the scene is seen "instantaneously" to extraordinary accuracy, and any laboratory retardation proposal must be checked against this cancellation (momentum conservation forbids an isolated oscillating mass dipole, so the support/recoil masses must be included). I could not verify how the Paranjape proposal handles this.

## 8. Do pressure, stress, heat and field energy gravitate measurably for laboratory bodies?

### Takeaway
Only one dedicated laboratory experiment (Kreuzer 1968) tests whether active gravitational mass equals passive mass across compositions, at 5×10⁻⁵; the lunar-ranging argument of Bartlett & Van Buren is far tighter. In GR pressure does source gravity (ρ + 3p/c²), but for a static confined body wall stresses cancel the pressure contribution exactly (Tolman paradox; Ehlers et al. 2005), and for laboratory bodies the pressure, stress and heat contributions are ~10⁻¹² of the rest mass, far below anything measured directly.

### Cited Findings
- **Kreuzer 1968**, "Experimental measurement of the equivalence of active and passive gravitational mass", Phys. Rev. 169, 1007–1012. A solid body was floated in a fluid at near-neutral buoyancy so the displaced masses were nearly equal; result: the active-to-passive mass ratios of bromine (dominant in the fluid) and fluorine (dominant in the solid) differ by less than about 5 parts in 10⁵. A 2025 paper calls it "the only dedicated laboratory experiment probing this principle"; Morrison & Hill's 1973 reanalysis confirmed the conclusion. — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/4b7536985f9ea69579425f6a5f56ab71); [arXiv:2503.16198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16198); [Peebles, "Robert Dicke and the naissance of experimental gravity physics", arXiv:1603.06474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06474); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/7364957)
- **Bartlett & Van Buren 1986.** If the Moon's composition is not spherically symmetric (iron-rich vs aluminium-rich regions), a difference in active/passive mass ratio between the materials would give the Moon a self-acceleration; lunar laser ranging bounds this tightly. — [arXiv:2503.16198 (review of the argument)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16198)
- **Ehlers, Ozsváth, Schücking & Shang 2005**, "Pressure as a source of gravity", Phys. Rev. D 72, 124003, arXiv:gr-qc/0510041. "The active mass density in Einstein's theory of gravitation in the analog of Poisson's equation in a local inertial system is proportional to ρ + 3p/c². Here ρ is the density of energy and p its pressure for a perfect fluid. By using exact solutions of Einstein's field equations in the static case we study whether the pressure term contributes towards the mass." Tolman's paradox: a static spherical box whose contents undergo an internal transformation (e.g. matter–antimatter into radiation) changes its pressure, apparently changing the active mass; Misner & Putnam showed that the stresses in the walls needed to confine the contents and keep the field static compensate, but neglected the influence of the gravitational field on the cancellation, which this paper addresses with exact solutions. Companion: Ehlers, Ozsváth & Schücking, "Active mass under pressure" (arXiv:gr-qc/0505040). — [arXiv:gr-qc/0510041](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0510041); [MPG PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_150943_1/component/file_150942/124003.pdf); [arXiv:gr-qc/0505040](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0505040)

### Inferences
- **[D] Orders of magnitude for laboratory bodies.** 3p/c² for air at 1 atm is 3.3×10⁻¹² kg/m³ (2.8×10⁻¹² of the air density); for steel stressed at 1 GPa it is 3.3×10⁻⁸ kg/m³ (4×10⁻¹² of the density); heating water by 100 K adds ΔE/c² = 4.7×10⁻¹² of its mass. All are seven orders of magnitude below Kreuzer's 5×10⁻⁵ laboratory bound, so no laboratory experiment has directly seen stress, pressure or heat gravitate; the evidence is indirect (equivalence-principle tests of passive mass for binding energies; astrophysical/cosmological pressure effects).
- For the camera: to imaging accuracy the source is the rest-mass density ρ(x, t); corrections from stress/heat are ≲10⁻¹¹ and, for static confined systems, the volume-integrated pressure and wall-stress contributions cancel (Tolman/Misner–Putnam/Ehlers et al.). A transient pressure change inside a rigid container therefore produces no monopole change, only tiny higher-order effects.

## 9. What a LIGO-grade pixel would already register from everyday objects

### Takeaway
Published LIGO noise studies and calibrator hardware show that kg-to-100 kg objects moving non-uniformly within ~1–10 m are already at or above the 10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² level in the 10–30 Hz band, and deliberately driven kg-scale rotors at ~1–2 m are 10³–10⁴ times above it; in tidal-gradient terms a LIGO-grade pixel is ~10⁻⁹ E/√Hz, about ten orders of magnitude beyond any gradiometer used for imaging today.

### Cited Findings
- **Walking person:** √S_h ∼ 0.6×10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² (f/10 Hz)⁻⁶ (r/10 m)⁻³ for 2.5–25 Hz, within a factor 3; humans must stay ≳10 m from a test mass for advanced-LIGO-level sensitivity; door slams and fist blows on walls are also controlled by the 10 m zone. — [Thorne & Winstein, arXiv:gr-qc/9810016](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9810016); [Thorne Nobel lecture](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/pdf/10.1103/RevModPhys.90.040503)
- **Tumbleweed (0.1 kg, wind-driven):** SNR ≈ 4 at 10 Hz in advanced LIGO when passing; SNR in the hundreds if it strikes the building; reference noise S_h ≈ 2×10⁻⁴⁵ Hz⁻¹ (√S_h ≈ 4.5×10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ²) at 10 Hz. — [Creighton, arXiv:gr-qc/0007050](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0007050)
- **Sonic booms / atmospheric shocks and airborne debris:** produce detectable transients; steady infrasound and advected temperature fluctuations are within about one order of magnitude of the advanced-detector floor. — [Creighton thesis](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/10400/2/Creighton_t_2000.pdf); [arXiv:gr-qc/0007050](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0007050)
- **Ground (seismic Rayleigh waves):** transfer coefficient β′ ≈ 0.35–0.6 at quiet times and 0.15–1.4 at noisy times; close to the advanced-LIGO noise near 10 Hz. — [Hughes & Thorne, arXiv:gr-qc/9806018](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9806018)
- **Uniformly moving objects (cars, people, air masses):** negligible above f ≈ v/(2πd) because of exponential suppression. — [Harms et al., arXiv:1308.2074](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2074)
- **Rotating kg-scale masses at 1.3–1.9 m (NCal):** routinely detected lines used for 0.12–0.17% absolute calibration of Virgo; LIGO rotor with ~1.06 kg tungsten cylinders predicted to <1%; signal ∝ d⁻⁴. — [Virgo O3 NCal, arXiv:2011.03728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03728); [Virgo O4 NCal, arXiv:2406.10028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10028); [Ross et al., arXiv:2107.00141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00141)
- **Bar-detector precedent:** a small rotating quadrupole gave 20× the noise of the 2270 kg Explorer antenna (1991); a vibrating bar's 1660 Hz field was detected by Weber-type apparatus (1967); a 42 Hz beam's field is measured with sub-picometre resolution (2022). — [Astone et al. 1991](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/BF01558552); [Sinsky & Weber 1967](https://scilit.com/publications/4666222ce9dc99c0f54179cc161e9002); [Brack et al., arXiv:2201.03420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03420)
- **Smallest source measured dynamically:** a 90 mg gold sphere modulated at 12.7 mHz, resolved to 4×10⁻¹² m/s² (statistical, 350 h). — [Westphal et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03250-7)
- **Largest/farthest natural source:** Mw 9.1 earthquake at 1,000–2,000 km, nm/s²-level prompt signal before the P wave. — [Vallée et al. 2017](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aao0746)
- **Today's imaging gradiometers for comparison:** 20 E per point (atom-interferometric tunnel survey), ~13–15 E/√Hz airborne, ~1 E at 10 s (Hughes RGG). — [Stray et al. 2022](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/quantum-sensing-for-gravity-cartography); [Geoscience Australia](https://www.ga.gov.au/bigobj/GA16642.pdf); [NASA NTRS 19830002134](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19830002134/downloads/19830002134.pdf)

### Inferences
- **[D] Conversion rules for the camera.** (i) Local tidal pixel (size ≪ r): h̃ = (E_xx − E_yy)/ω²; gradient-equivalent noise = (2πf)²√S_h: 10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² corresponds to 3.9×10⁻²⁰ s⁻² Hz⁻¹ᐟ² at 10 Hz (3.9×10⁻¹¹ E/√Hz) and 3.9×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻² Hz⁻¹ᐟ² at 100 Hz (3.9×10⁻⁹ E/√Hz). (ii) Real LIGO geometry (arm L ≫ r): h̃ = δã/(ω² L) with δa the acceleration of the near test mass.
- **[D] Generic point-mass estimates** (mass m oscillating with amplitude ξ along the line of sight at distance r ≫ ξ): δa = 2Gmξ/r³, δΓ_rr = 6Gmξ/r⁴. Example: m = 1 kg, ξ = 1 cm, r = 1 m, f = 10 Hz gives δa = 1.3×10⁻¹² m/s² and δΓ = 4.0×10⁻¹² s⁻² (4×10⁻³ E); a local tidal pixel reads h ≈ δΓ/ω² ≈ 1.0×10⁻¹⁵ (times an orientation factor of order one, up to 1.5 since E_xx − E_yy = 1.5 δΓ_rr for a source on the x arm axis), a 4 km LIGO reads h ≈ 8×10⁻²⁰. Against 10⁻²³ Hz⁻¹ᐟ² this is SNR ~10⁸ (local pixel) or ~10⁴ (LIGO geometry) in one second. The same object at r = 10 m: 10⁻⁴ of the gradient (r⁻⁴), still SNR ~10⁴ per √s for the local pixel.
- **[D] Walking person, other forms:** 9.5×10⁻¹⁷ m s⁻²/√Hz at 10 m and 10 Hz (from Thorne–Winstein with L = 4 km); ≈10⁻¹³ m s⁻²/√Hz at 1 m.
- **[D] Two-mass rotor:** a₂f = 9Gmr²/(2d⁴); for m = 1 kg, r = 0.1 m, d = 1.3 m: 1.05×10⁻¹² m/s², h ≈ 2×10⁻²⁰ at 20 Hz on a 3 km arm (assumed illustrative parameters).
- **[D] What is *not* registered:** steady motion (suppressed as e^{−2πfd/v}); radiation from the object (down by (r/ƛ)⁴ ~ 10⁻²³ at 10 m, 10 Hz); frame-drag B_ij (down by r/ƛ ~ 10⁻⁶); propagation delay (phase lag (r/ƛ)⁵/45 ~ 10⁻³⁰ rad); pressure/heat contributions to the source (~10⁻¹² of rest mass).
- **[D] Imaging consequence:** detection is easy by many orders of magnitude, but resolution is set by Δx ≈ π z / ln(SNR) (Section 5): SNR 10⁴ gives Δx ≈ z/3, SNR 10⁸ gives Δx ≈ z/6, SNR 10²⁰ gives Δx ≈ z/15.
